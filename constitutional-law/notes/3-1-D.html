Module 03 · Fundamental Rights

Suspension of Fundamental Rights

Constitutional Law of India – I · Topic 3.1 D

Suspension of Fundamental Rights and Their Application to the Armed Forces

Introduction: Fundamental Rights are guaranteed under Part III of the Indian Constitution. They protect individual freedom, equality and dignity. However, the Constitution permits certain restrictions on these rights during a National Emergency. It also allows Parliament to restrict or modify their application to members of the Armed Forces, even during normal times, under specific constitutional provisions.

1. Suspension of Fundamental Rights During National Emergency

Under Article 352, the President can proclaim a National Emergency when the security of India or any part of its territory is threatened by war, external aggression or armed rebellion. During such an emergency, the Constitution provides two important provisions regarding Fundamental Rights.

Article 358: Suspension of Article 19

Article 358 becomes applicable automatically when a National Emergency is declared on the grounds of war or external aggression. During such an emergency, Article 19 does not restrict the State's power to make laws or take executive action covered by this provision. However, the law must satisfy the conditions prescribed under Article 358. This provision does not apply to an emergency declared on the ground of armed rebellion.

Article 359: Suspension of Constitutional Remedies

Under Article 359, the President may issue an order suspending the right to approach courts for the enforcement of specified Fundamental Rights during a National Emergency. The order may operate for the duration of the emergency or for a shorter period. However, Articles 20 and 21 cannot be included in such an order. Article 20 protects against certain forms of criminal punishment, while Article 21 protects life and personal liberty.

Thus, Article 358 concerns Article 19, whereas Article 359 concerns the enforcement of specified Fundamental Rights through courts.

2. Can Fundamental Rights Be Restricted for the Armed Forces During Normal Times?

Yes. Article 33 empowers Parliament to determine by law the extent to which Fundamental Rights may be restricted or abrogated in their application to certain categories of persons, even during normal times.

These categories include:

1. Members of the Armed Forces.

2. Members of forces responsible for maintaining public order.

3. Persons employed in intelligence or counter-intelligence organisations established by the State.

4. Persons employed in or connected with specified communication systems established for these forces or organisations.

The main purpose of Article 33 is to ensure the proper performance of duties and maintain discipline. For example, the law may restrict certain freedoms of military personnel to maintain discipline, prevent breaches of security and ensure obedience to lawful commands. However, these restrictions must be made by Parliament through law; they are not an unrestricted power of the executive.

Conclusion: Fundamental Rights are essential to Indian democracy and cannot be suspended arbitrarily. Articles 358 and 359 provide limited arrangements during a National Emergency, while Article 33 permits Parliament to restrict the application of certain rights to specified forces during normal times. These provisions seek to balance individual freedom with national security and discipline.
