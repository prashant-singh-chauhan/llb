Concept of Fundamental Rights

1. Introduction and Meaning: Fundamental Rights are the basic rights guaranteed by the Indian Constitution to protect individual freedom, equality and dignity. They are provided under Part III (Articles 12–35) of the Constitution.

2. Nature of Fundamental Rights: Fundamental Rights are constitutional rights that protect individuals against arbitrary actions of the State. They are enforceable by courts. However, some rights are subject to reasonable restrictions and constitutional limitations. Certain rights are available to all persons, while some are available only to citizens.

3. Purpose of Fundamental Rights: The main purpose of these rights is to protect individual liberty, prevent misuse of government power, eliminate discrimination and establish a democratic society based on justice and equality.

4. Importance of Fundamental Rights:

Protection of Freedom: They protect essential freedoms and personal liberty.

Promotion of Equality: They ensure equality before the law and prohibit certain forms of discrimination.

Protection of Human Dignity: They help individuals live with dignity and self-respect.

Strengthening Democracy: They allow people to exercise their rights freely and participate in democratic life.

Judicial Protection: Article 32 allows individuals to approach the Supreme Court to enforce their Fundamental Rights. High Courts can also provide remedies under Article 226.

Conclusion: Fundamental Rights are essential for Indian democracy. They protect individual liberty, promote equality and ensure that government power is exercised according to the Constitution.

1. Scheme of Fundamental Rights

Introduction: Fundamental Rights are the basic rights guaranteed by the Indian Constitution to protect individual freedom, equality and dignity. They are provided under Part III (Articles 12 to 35) of the Constitution. Official text of the Constitution.

The six categories of Fundamental Rights are as follows:

1. Right to Equality (Articles 14–18): It provides equality before the law and prohibits untouchability and the abolition of titles.

2. Right to Freedom (Articles 19–22): It protects important freedoms, life, personal liberty and safeguards against certain forms of arrest.

3. Right against Exploitation (Articles 23–24): It prohibits human trafficking, forced labour and certain forms of child labour.

4. Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25–28): It protects religious freedom, subject to constitutional restrictions.

5. Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29–30): It protects the cultural and educational interests of minorities.

6. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32): It allows individuals to approach the Supreme Court for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

Conclusion: The scheme of Fundamental Rights protects people against arbitrary government action and promotes democracy, liberty, equality and justice. These rights are enforceable through courts, subject to constitutional provisions.
