Module 03 · Fundamental Rights

Equality of Opportunity in matters of public employment. Birth

Constitutional Law of India – I · Topic 3.2 A3 - Article 16

1. Introduction

Article 16 of the Constitution of India guarantees equality of opportunity to all citizens in matters relating to employment or appointment to any office under the State. It aims to prevent discrimination in public employment and ensure fairness in government services.

Grounds of Prohibited Discrimination

  • Religion
  • Race
  • Caste
  • Sex
  • Descent
  • Place of birth
  • Residence

Article 16(1): It provides equality of opportunity for all citizens in matters relating to employment or appointment to any office under the State.

Article 16(2): It provides that no citizen shall be discriminated against or be ineligible for any public employment on the grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence or any of them.

Article 16(3): It empowers Parliament to make laws prescribing residence requirements within a State or Union Territory for certain classes of public employment. A State Legislature cannot independently exercise this power.

Article 16(4): It provides that the State may make provisions for the reservation of appointments or posts in favour of any backward class of citizens which, in the opinion of the State, is not adequately represented in the services under the State.

Article 16(4A): It empowers the State to provide reservation in matters of promotion, with consequential seniority, for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes that are not adequately represented in the services under the State. Such reservation is subject to constitutional conditions.

Article 16(4B): It allows the State to treat unfilled reserved vacancies of a particular year as a separate class of vacancies to be carried forward to subsequent years. These backlog vacancies are not counted with the current year's vacancies for calculating the 50% ceiling applicable to reservations in that year.

Article 16(5): It provides that a law may require the holder of an office connected with the affairs of a religious or denominational institution, or a member of its governing body, to belong to a particular religion or denomination.

Article 16(6): It empowers the State to provide reservation in appointments or posts for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS), other than the classes mentioned in Article 16(4). Such reservation may be up to 10% of the posts in each category, in addition to existing reservations, subject to constitutional conditions.

2. Important Points

Purpose: Article 16 promotes equality, fairness and social justice in public employment.

Reservation: It permits reservation for backward classes that are inadequately represented, as well as reservation in promotion for SCs and STs under Article 16(4A), subject to constitutional requirements.

Economic Criteria: Article 16(6), inserted by the 103rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019, permits reservation for economically weaker sections.

3. Conclusion

Article 16 is an important constitutional safeguard that ensures equality of opportunity in government employment. It prohibits unfair discrimination while permitting reservation and certain special provisions to improve representation and promote social justice.

The nature and extent of 'Reservation' in matters of public employment has been considered by the Supreme Court of India in several cases. Examine the statement in the light of major judicial pronouncements. (2019)

1. Introduction:

Reservation in public employment is an important means of achieving social justice and equality in India. Article 16 of the Indian Constitution guarantees equality of opportunity in public employment. Article 16(4) permits reservation for backward classes inadequately represented in government services. Article 16(4A) deals with reservation in promotion for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs).

2. Major Judicial Pronouncements:

(A) State of Kerala v. N.M. Thomas (1976)

Facts: Certain SC/ST employees received temporary relaxation from passing departmental tests for promotion. Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld the relaxation. Principle: Reservation and special concessions are measures to achieve real equality, not merely equality in form.

(B) Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)

Facts: The case challenged the Central Government's decision to provide 27% reservation to Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in government employment. Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld the 27% reservation for OBCs, subject to exclusion of the creamy layer. Principle: Reservation under Article 16(4) should ordinarily not exceed 50%. The Court also held that Article 16(4) does not permit reservation in promotion, subject to its directions protecting existing arrangements for a limited period.

(C) R.K. Sabharwal v. State of Punjab (1995)

Facts: The dispute concerned the implementation of reservation through a roster in government services. Judgment: The Supreme Court held that reservation should be calculated according to the total sanctioned posts in a cadre. Principle: The post-based roster must stop operating once the prescribed reservation quota is achieved.

(D) M. Nagaraj v. Union of India (2006)

Facts: The constitutional amendments permitting reservation in promotion for SCs and STs were challenged. Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld the amendments, subject to constitutional conditions. Principle: The State must consider inadequate representation, administrative efficiency, and relevant quantifiable data before providing reservation in promotion.

(E) Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta (2018)

Facts: The Court examined the conditions laid down in M. Nagaraj for reservation in promotion. Judgment: It held that the State need not collect quantifiable data to prove the backwardness of SCs and STs. The creamy-layer principle must also be applied. Principle: Reservation in promotion must satisfy constitutional requirements, including adequate representation and administrative efficiency.

(F) Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022)

Facts: The validity of the 103rd Constitutional Amendment providing reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) was challenged. Judgment: By a 3:2 majority, the Supreme Court upheld the amendment. Principle: Up to 10% reservation for EWS under Article 16(6), in addition to existing reservations, is constitutionally valid.

3. Conclusion:

The Supreme Court has recognised reservation as an instrument of social justice while placing constitutional limits on its implementation. These judgments establish that reservation must promote genuine equality, ensure adequate representation, and respect administrative efficiency and constitutional principles.
