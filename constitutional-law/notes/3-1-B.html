Module 03 · Fundamental Rights

Who can claim Fundamental Rights

Constitutional Law of India – I · Topic 3.1 B

1. Who Can Claim Fundamental Rights?

Introduction: Fundamental Rights are the basic rights guaranteed under Part III (Articles 12–35) of the Indian Constitution. They protect freedom, equality and human dignity. However, all Fundamental Rights are not available to everyone in the same way. The right to claim a particular Fundamental Right depends on the wording and nature of the relevant constitutional provision.

1. Indian Citizens: Indian citizens can claim all Fundamental Rights applicable to them under the Constitution. Certain rights are available exclusively to citizens. For example, Article 15 prohibits discrimination by the State on specified grounds, Article 16 provides equality of opportunity in public employment, and Article 19 guarantees important freedoms such as speech and expression, peaceful assembly and movement, subject to constitutional restrictions. Article 29(1) also protects the cultural interests of sections of citizens.

2. Foreigners and Other Persons: Many Fundamental Rights are available to every person within the territory of India, including foreign nationals. Article 14 guarantees equality before the law and equal protection of the laws. Article 20 provides protection in respect of conviction for offences, while Article 21 protects life and personal liberty. Article 25 protects freedom of conscience and religion. However, certain constitutional exceptions apply to particular rights and circumstances.

3. Companies and Legal Persons: Certain legal entities, such as companies and corporations, can claim Fundamental Rights where the language and nature of the right permit it. For example, a company may claim protection under Article 14. However, a company is not considered an Indian citizen and cannot independently claim the freedoms guaranteed exclusively to citizens under Article 19.

4. Religious and Cultural Groups: Religious denominations can claim applicable rights under Article 26. Sections of citizens can claim protection under Article 29, while religious and linguistic minorities have the right under Article 30 to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice, subject to constitutional provisions.

5. Rights Against Private Individuals: Fundamental Rights generally protect individuals against unconstitutional State action. However, certain rights also operate against private individuals. For example, Article 17 abolishes untouchability, Article 23 prohibits human trafficking and forced labour, and Article 24 prohibits the employment of children below fourteen years in factories, mines and other hazardous employment.

6. Enforcement of Fundamental Rights: Under Article 32, a person can approach the Supreme Court for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights. Article 226 empowers High Courts to issue writs for enforcing Fundamental Rights and for other legal purposes. In appropriate cases, public interest litigation may also be used to protect the rights of persons who cannot approach the courts themselves.

Important Case: In State Trading Corporation of India Ltd. v. Commercial Tax Officer (1963), the Supreme Court held that a corporation is not a citizen and cannot independently claim the rights reserved for citizens under Article 19.

Conclusion: Thus, Indian citizens, foreigners and certain legal entities can claim applicable Fundamental Rights. These rights protect individuals against injustice, promote equality and freedom, and strengthen constitutional democracy.
