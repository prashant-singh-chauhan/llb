Module 03 · Fundamental Rights

Right to Education (Article 21A)

Constitutional Law of India – I · Topic 3.2 E

Introduction:

Education is essential for the development of an individual and society. It helps people gain knowledge, develop their abilities, and live with dignity. The Constitution of India recognises the importance of education as a fundamental right under Article 21A. This right makes it the responsibility of the State to provide free and compulsory education to children of a specified age group.

Meaning of the Fundamental Right to Education:

The Fundamental Right to Education means that every child has a legal right to receive free and compulsory education. Article 21A was added to the Constitution by the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002.

Article 21A provides that the State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children between the ages of 6 and 14 years, in such manner as the State may determine by law.

The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002 introduced the Fundamental Right to Education under Article 21A of the Constitution of India.

To give effect to this right, Parliament enacted the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009, commonly known as the RTE Act. It came into force on 1 April 2010.

Free education means that no child should be prevented from completing elementary education because of financial difficulties.

Compulsory education means that the government must ensure the admission, attendance, and completion of elementary education by children of the prescribed age.

Importance of the Right to Education:

1. Development of children: It helps children develop their knowledge, skills, and personality.

2. Equality of opportunity: It provides children from poor and disadvantaged families with an opportunity to receive education.

3. Removal of illiteracy: It helps reduce illiteracy and promotes awareness among citizens.

4. Democracy: Education enables citizens to understand their rights and duties and participate effectively in democratic life.

5. Social justice: It helps reduce social and economic inequalities.

6. Human dignity: Education improves employment opportunities and enables individuals to live a more dignified life.

7. National development: Educated citizens contribute to the economic, social, and cultural progress of the country.

Age of Children and Period of Enforcement:

Article 21A grants the Right to Education to children between 6 and 14 years of age. It covers elementary education. The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002, inserted Article 21A, while the RTE Act, 2009, provided the legal framework for implementing this right. The Act came into force on 1 April 2010.

Contribution of the Judiciary:

The judiciary played an important role in recognising education as a fundamental right and encouraging Parliament to provide constitutional protection to it.

1. Mohini Jain vs. State of Karnataka (1992)

Facts: The case concerned the charging of high fees, commonly known as capitation fees, for admission to a private medical college.

Judgment: The Supreme Court held that the right to education flows from the right to life under Article 21.

Principle of Law: Education is essential for living with dignity, and the State must work towards providing educational opportunities.

2. Unni Krishnan, J.P. vs. State of Andhra Pradesh (1993)

Facts: The case concerned the regulation of admissions and fees in private professional educational institutions.

Judgment: The Supreme Court held that children have a fundamental right to free education up to the age of 14 years. Education beyond that age was subject to the economic capacity and development of the State.

Principle of Law: The right to education is part of Article 21, at least up to the age of 14 years.

3. State of Tamil Nadu vs. K. Shyam Sundar (2011)

Facts: The case concerned the validity of the Tamil Nadu Uniform System of School Education Act, 2010, which aimed to introduce a uniform system of school education in the State. The dispute arose over the implementation of this common educational system.

Judgment: The Supreme Court considered the importance of providing equal educational opportunities to children and examined the implementation of the uniform education system. The case highlighted the need to ensure that educational policies serve the interests of children and provide equality in education.

Principle of Law: The case emphasised the importance of equality and the welfare of children in the field of education. It supports the broader objective of ensuring fair educational opportunities for all children.

These judgments highlighted the need for express constitutional recognition of the right to education. Consequently, Parliament enacted the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002, inserting Article 21A.

Conclusion:

The Fundamental Right to Education is a major step towards equality, social justice, and national development. Article 21A guarantees free and compulsory education to children aged 6–14 years. The judiciary laid the foundation for this right through important judgments, while Parliament gave it explicit constitutional recognition through the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002.
