Module 03 · Fundamental Rights

Abolition of Titles

Constitutional Law of India – I · Topic 3.2 A5 - Article 18

Introduction

Article 18 of the Indian Constitution provides for the abolition of titles. It prohibits the State from conferring titles, except military or academic distinctions. Its main purpose is to promote equality and prevent the creation of artificial social classes based on titles.

2. Meaning of Abolition of Titles

Abolition of titles means that the State cannot confer titles that create a special social status or imply superiority over other citizens. Every citizen should enjoy equal status without privileges based on such titles. However, military and academic distinctions are permitted under Article 18(1).

3. Main Provisions of Article 18

1. Article 18(1) – Prohibition on Titles: The State cannot confer any title except military or academic distinctions.

2. Article 18(2) – Foreign Titles: An Indian citizen cannot accept any title from a foreign State.

3. Article 18(3) – Foreign Titles and Non-Citizens: A person who is not an Indian citizen and holds an office of profit or trust under the State cannot accept a title from a foreign State without the President's consent.

4. Article 18(4) – Gifts and Offices from Foreign States: A person holding an office of profit or trust under the State cannot accept any present, emolument (payment or benefit), or office from or under a foreign State without the President's consent.

4. Exceptions to Article 18

1. Military Distinctions: Military distinctions, such as recognised military ranks and honours, are permitted.

2. Academic Distinctions: Academic distinctions, such as recognised educational honours, are permitted.

3. National Awards: Awards such as Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan and Padma Shri are not considered titles under Article 18. However, they must not be used as prefixes or suffixes to a person's name.

5. Illustrations of Article 18

1. Prohibition on Titles: The government cannot confer titles such as "Rai Bahadur" or "Sir" to create a special social status for a person.

2. Academic Distinctions: A university may confer academic degrees or distinctions in recognition of educational achievement.

3. National Awards: A person receiving the Padma Shri may mention the award as an honour but cannot use it as a prefix or suffix to their name.

4. Foreign Titles: An Indian citizen cannot accept a title from a foreign State, even if that title is offered in recognition of personal achievement.

5. Gifts from Foreign States: A person holding an office of profit or trust under the State must obtain the President's consent before accepting a gift or office covered by Article 18(4) from a foreign State.

6. Important Judicial Pronouncement

Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India (1995):

Facts: Two public interest petitions were filed challenging the conferment of national awards, such as Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan and Padma Shri. The petitioners argued that these awards violated Article 18 of the Indian Constitution, which prohibits the conferment of titles.

Judgment: The Supreme Court held that national awards are not titles prohibited under Article 18(1). However, these awards must not be used as prefixes or suffixes to a person's name.

Principle: The State may recognise exceptional merit and distinguished service through national awards, provided that such awards do not create artificial social distinctions or become titles. This principle promotes equality and upholds the spirit of Article 18.

Indira Jaising v. Supreme Court of India (AIR 2017 SC 5017)

Facts: Indira Jaising, a Senior Advocate, challenged the system of designation of Senior Advocates. She argued that the existing procedure lacked transparency and could be arbitrary. The case also raised the question of whether the designation of Senior Advocates violated Article 18 of the Constitution, which prohibits titles.

Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld the constitutional validity of Section 16 of the Advocates Act, 1961. It held that the designation of an advocate as a "Senior Advocate" is a distinction and recognition of professional merit, not a title prohibited by Article 18. The Court also emphasised the need for proper guidelines for designation.

Principle: The designation of "Senior Advocate" is constitutionally valid because it recognises professional ability, standing at the Bar, and special knowledge or experience in law. Such recognition does not violate Article 18.

7. Scope and Importance of Article 18

Article 18 promotes equality by preventing the creation of social superiority through State-conferred titles. It also restricts the acceptance of certain titles, gifts, benefits and offices from foreign States. Military and academic distinctions remain permitted under the Constitution.

Conclusion: Article 18 is an important part of the Right to Equality. It abolishes titles that create artificial social distinctions and promotes equality, dignity and equal status among the citizens of India.
