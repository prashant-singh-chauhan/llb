Preamble of the Constitution

Constitutional Law of India – I · Topic 2.1

1. Preamble of India

WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:

JUSTICE, social, economic and political;

LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;

EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;

and to promote among them all

FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;

IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.

2. Meaning of Preamble

1. Sovereign: Sovereign means that India is completely independent and has the power to make its own decisions. No foreign country can control India's internal or external affairs. India can make its own laws, frame policies, maintain relations with other countries and protect its national interests. It also has the right to defend its freedom and territory. Thus, sovereignty means that India is free to govern itself without being under the control of another country.

2. Socialist: Socialist means that the government aims to reduce social and economic inequalities and promote the welfare of all people. It seeks to ensure that everyone gets fair opportunities to improve their lives. The government works to reduce poverty, provide education, improve healthcare and protect weaker sections of society. Indian socialism does not completely remove private property or private businesses. Instead, it aims to create a more equal society where wealth and opportunities are not unfairly concentrated in a few hands.

3. Secular: Secular means that India has no official state religion and treats all religions equally. Every person has the freedom to follow, practise and spread their religion, subject to constitutional limits. The government cannot unfairly favour or discriminate against citizens because of their religious beliefs. People are free to follow any religion or choose not to follow one. Secularism promotes religious tolerance, peace and respect among different communities and helps maintain unity in India's diverse society.

4. Democratic: Democratic means that the ultimate power of government rests with the people. In India, citizens who meet the legal requirements have the right to vote and elect their representatives. Elections are held regularly, allowing people to change their government peacefully. The government must work according to the Constitution and remain answerable to the people. Democracy also protects individual rights, equality and freedom. It encourages public participation in decision-making and ensures that the government cannot exercise power according to its wishes alone.

5. Republic: Republic means that the head of the country is not a hereditary ruler, such as a king or queen. In India, the President is the constitutional head of the State and is elected indirectly for a fixed term. The position is not inherited through family membership. Every eligible citizen can aspire to hold public office according to the law. The idea of a republic supports political equality and ensures that public positions are based on constitutional rules rather than birth or royal family background.

6. Justice: Justice means giving every person fair and equal treatment in society. The Preamble promises social, economic and political justice. Social justice means ending discrimination based on caste, religion, gender or other grounds. Economic justice aims to reduce unfair economic inequalities and provide fair opportunities to earn a living. Political justice means that citizens should have equal political rights and opportunities to participate in government. Justice seeks to protect people's rights, prevent exploitation and create a fair society where everyone can live with dignity.

7. Liberty: Liberty means freedom for individuals to think, express their opinions, follow their beliefs and practise their religion. The Preamble promises liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship. It allows people to express their views and make personal choices without unnecessary interference. However, liberty does not mean unlimited freedom; it must be exercised within the limits of the Constitution and law. Liberty protects individual dignity and encourages people to develop their personalities while respecting the rights and freedoms of others.

8. Equality: Equality means that all people should enjoy equal status and opportunities. No person should receive unfair treatment because of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth. Equality before the law means that everyone is subject to the law and deserves equal legal protection. Equality of opportunity means that people should have a fair chance to progress in life. The Constitution also permits special measures for disadvantaged groups to address historical inequalities. Therefore, equality aims to build a society based on fairness, dignity and equal rights.

9. Fraternity: Fraternity means a feeling of brotherhood, friendship and unity among all citizens. It encourages people to respect one another despite differences in religion, language, caste, culture or region. The Preamble states that fraternity must ensure the dignity of every individual and the unity and integrity of the nation. It reminds citizens to live peacefully, support one another and avoid discrimination and hatred. Fraternity strengthens national integration and helps create a society where diversity is respected and everyone feels that they belong to India.

3. 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976

The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 added three words to the Preamble of the Indian Constitution: Socialist, Secular and Integrity. It made two important changes to the Preamble. First, the words “Sovereign Democratic Republic” were changed to “Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic”. Second, the words “unity of the Nation” were changed to “unity and integrity of the Nation”.

1. Socialist: It means reducing social and economic inequalities and promoting public welfare. The government aims to provide equal opportunities and improve the living conditions of all citizens.

2. Secular: It means that India has no official state religion. The government treats all religions equally, and citizens are free to follow and practise their religions, subject to constitutional limits.

3. Integrity: It means maintaining the unity, wholeness and national solidarity of India. It encourages citizens to remain united despite differences in religion, language and culture.

Conclusion: These changes strengthened the constitutional commitment to social welfare, religious equality and national unity. Therefore, the 42nd Amendment is important in the history of the Indian Constitution.

4. Salient Features of the Preamble of the Indian Constitution
OR
4. “Preamble Gives Direction and Purpose to the Constitution.” Describe.

1. Source of Authority: The Preamble begins with the words “We, the People of India”, which show that the Constitution derives its authority from the people.

2. Nature of the Indian State: It declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic. These terms describe India's independent, welfare-oriented, religiously neutral and democratic character.

3. Sovereign: India is independent and free to make its own decisions without foreign control.

4. Socialist: It aims to reduce social and economic inequalities and promote public welfare.

5. Secular: The State treats all religions equally and protects religious freedom.

6. Democratic: The people elect their representatives through regular elections.

7. Republic: The head of the State is elected, not a hereditary ruler.

8. Objectives: The Preamble promises Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity to promote fairness, freedom, equal opportunities, brotherhood and individual dignity.

9. Unity and Integrity: It promotes national unity and the integrity of India.

10. Constitutional Amendment: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, added the words “Socialist”, “Secular” and “Integrity” to the Preamble.

11. Date of Adoption: The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949.

Conclusion: The Preamble reflects the basic ideals and objectives of the Indian Constitution and guides the government in building a just, free, equal and united society.

5. Can Preamble be part of Indian Constitution? Explain in very brief.

Introduction

The Preamble is the introductory part of the Indian Constitution. It explains the basic ideals, objectives and principles of the Constitution. It begins with the words “We, the People of India” and declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic. It also promises Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity to all citizens.

Importance and Legal Status of the Preamble

The Preamble helps us understand the aims and philosophy of the Constitution. It acts as a guide for the government and the judiciary while interpreting constitutional provisions. It reflects the goals that India seeks to achieve.

The Preamble is a part of the Indian Constitution, but it is not directly enforceable by courts. Citizens cannot approach a court only because a particular objective mentioned in the Preamble has not been fulfilled. It does not independently give powers to the government. However, it helps courts understand the meaning of constitutional provisions when their interpretation is unclear.

The Preamble can be amended under Article 368, but Parliament cannot change it in a way that destroys the basic structure of the Constitution.

Important Judicial Decisions

1. Berubari Union Case (1960): In this case, the Supreme Court held that the Preamble was not a part of the Constitution. However, it recognised the Preamble as a guide to understanding the intentions of the Constitution-makers.

2. Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973): In this landmark case, the Supreme Court rejected the earlier view and held that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution. The Court also established that Parliament's power to amend the Constitution cannot destroy its basic structure.

Conclusion: Therefore, the Preamble is an important part of the Indian Constitution. It guides constitutional interpretation and expresses the fundamental ideals on which Indian democracy is based.

6. What Do You Mean by the Term ‘Secularism’?

Introduction

Secularism means that the State does not have an official religion and treats all religions equally. It gives every citizen the freedom to follow, practise and profess any religion. India is a secular country, and the Constitution protects religious freedom for all citizens.

Meaning of Secularism in India

The word “Secular” was added to the Preamble of the Indian Constitution by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976. However, the Constitution protected religious freedom even before this amendment.

1. No State Religion: India has no official religion. The government does not establish any religion as the religion of the country.

2. Religious Freedom: Article 25 provides freedom of conscience and the right to profess, practise and propagate religion, subject to constitutional restrictions.

3. Equality of Religions: The State must not unfairly discriminate against citizens on religious grounds.

4. Protection of Minorities: Articles 29 and 30 protect the cultural and educational rights of minorities.

5. Equal Respect: People of different religions can live together peacefully, and the State must protect their constitutional rights.

Importance of Secularism

Secularism promotes religious tolerance, equality, national unity and peaceful coexistence. It protects individual freedom and prevents religious discrimination.

Conclusion

Thus, secularism is an essential principle of Indian democracy. It ensures religious freedom and equal treatment while promoting unity among people of different faiths.
