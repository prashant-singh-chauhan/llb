Module 03 · Fundamental Rights

Specific Fundamental Rights

Constitutional Law of India – I · Topic 3.2

How many fundamental Rights are available there under the Indian Constitution? Write down very briefly.

1. Introduction:

Fundamental Rights are the basic rights guaranteed by the Constitution of India. They protect the freedom, equality, and dignity of individuals and are essential for the development of a democratic society.

2. Number of Fundamental Rights:

Originally, the Indian Constitution provided seven Fundamental Rights. However, the 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978, removed the Right to Property from the list of Fundamental Rights. Therefore, at present, there are six Fundamental Rights under Part III (Articles 12 to 35) of the Indian Constitution.

3. Six Fundamental Rights:

Right to Equality (Articles 14–18): Provides equality before the law and prohibits discrimination.

Right to Freedom (Articles 19–22): Guarantees important freedoms, including freedom of speech and expression.

Right against Exploitation (Articles 23–24): Prohibits human trafficking, forced labour, and certain forms of child labour.

Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25–28): Gives freedom to follow and practise religion, subject to constitutional limitations.

Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29–30): Protects the cultural and educational interests of minorities.

Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32): Allows individuals to approach the Supreme Court to enforce their Fundamental Rights.

4. Conclusion:

Thus, the Indian Constitution provides six Fundamental Rights to protect individual liberty, equality, and dignity and to ensure justice in a democratic country.
