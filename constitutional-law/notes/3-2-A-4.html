Module 03 · Fundamental Rights

Abolition of Untouchability Birth

Constitutional Law of India – I · Topic 3.2 A4 - Article 17

1. Introduction

Article 17 of the Indian Constitution provides for the abolition of untouchability. It declares that untouchability is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden. The enforcement of any disability arising out of untouchability is an offence punishable by law.

2. Meaning of Untouchability

Untouchability refers to the historical practice of imposing social disabilities on certain persons because of their caste. It includes treating people as inferior, denying them equal access to public places, and excluding them from social activities because of caste. The term is not defined in the Constitution, but its meaning has been understood in its historical and social context.

3. Main Features of Article 17

1. Complete Abolition: Article 17 completely abolishes untouchability and prohibits its practice in every form.

2. Punishable Offence: The enforcement of any disability arising from untouchability is punishable according to law.

3. Application to Private Individuals: The prohibition applies not only to the State but also to private individuals and organisations.

4. Protection of Human Dignity: Article 17 protects people against caste-based humiliation, exclusion and social discrimination.

4. Laws Related to Article 17

1. Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955: This Act provides punishment for the practice of untouchability and the enforcement of disabilities arising from it.

2. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989: This Act provides legal protection against specified offences and atrocities committed against members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.

5. Illustrations of Article 17

1. Public Wells: Preventing a person from drawing water from a public well because of caste is an example of prohibited untouchability.

2. Temples: Denying access to a public place of worship on the ground of caste-based untouchability violates the constitutional principle of equality.

3. Hotels and Shops: Refusing service to a person because of caste-based untouchability is prohibited by law.

4. Social Discrimination: Separating people or denying them ordinary social services because they belong to a particular caste may amount to the practice of untouchability.

6. Important Judicial Pronouncement

State of Karnataka v. Appa Balu Ingale (1993): In this case, persons belonging to a Scheduled Caste were prevented from drawing water from a borewell because of their caste. The Supreme Court strongly condemned the practice and emphasised that Article 17 must be effectively enforced. The judgment recognised that the abolition of untouchability is essential for establishing social equality and dignity.

7. Exceptions and Scope

Article 17 contains no express constitutional exception permitting untouchability. However, not every form of social separation automatically amounts to untouchability; the circumstances and connection with the historically practised caste-based discrimination must be considered.

Conclusion: Article 17 is an important Fundamental Right under the Right to Equality. It abolishes untouchability, protects human dignity and promotes social justice. It aims to establish an equal society free from caste-based exclusion and discrimination.
