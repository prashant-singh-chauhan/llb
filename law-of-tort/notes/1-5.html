Module 01 · Torts and Tortious Liability Introduction

Fault – Damnum sine injuria, Injuria sine damnum

Law of Torts & Consumer Protection · Topic 1.5

1. Damnum Sine Injuria (Damage Without Legal Injury)

Introduction:

Damnum Sine Injuria is an important legal maxim in the law of torts. It means actual damage or loss without violation of a legal right. According to this principle, a person may suffer financial loss or damage due to the lawful act of another person, but cannot claim compensation if no legal right has been violated.

This principle establishes that every loss does not give rise to a legal action. For a successful claim in tort, there must generally be a violation of a legal right.

Meaning of Damnum Sine Injuria

• Damnum: Means damage or loss.

• Sine: Means without.

• Injuria: Means violation of a legal right.

• Therefore, Damnum Sine Injuria means damage without violation of a legal right.

Essential Features of Damnum Sine Injuria

1. Actual Damage or Loss:

The plaintiff may suffer financial loss, loss of business or damage to property due to the act of another person.

2. No Violation of Legal Right:

The most important requirement is that the legal right of the plaintiff must not be violated by the defendant.

3. No Right to Compensation:

Even if the plaintiff suffers actual financial loss, they cannot claim compensation when no legal right has been violated.

4. Lawful Exercise of Rights:

A person is generally free to exercise their legal rights. If this lawful exercise causes loss to another person, it does not automatically create liability in tort.

5. No Cause of Action:

When there is damage without legal injury, the plaintiff generally has no legal right to bring an action for compensation.

Important Case Laws
1. Gloucester Grammar School Case (1410)

Facts: The defendant, a schoolteacher, opened a new school in the same locality where the plaintiff was already running a school. The defendant charged lower fees, due to which many students left the plaintiff's school and joined the new school. As a result, the plaintiff suffered financial loss.

Judgment: The Court held that the defendant was not liable because opening a new school and providing education at lower fees was a lawful act.

Principle of Law: Mere financial loss caused by lawful competition does not give rise to a legal action when no legal right has been violated.

2. Mogul Steamship Co. Ltd. v. McGregor, Gow & Co. (1892)

Facts: Several shipping companies formed an association and offered discounts to customers who used only their ships. They also reduced freight charges to attract business. As a result, a rival shipping company suffered financial loss.

Judgment: The House of Lords held that the defendants were not liable because they were pursuing their commercial interests through lawful methods.

Principle of Law: Lawful business competition does not become a tort merely because it causes financial loss to another person.

3. Chasemore v. Richards (1859)

Facts: The defendant dug a well on his land and extracted underground water. This reduced the water flowing into the river that supplied the plaintiff's mill. Consequently, the plaintiff suffered financial loss.

Judgment: The House of Lords held that the defendant was not liable because the underground water was percolating through the soil and the plaintiff had no legal right to prevent its extraction in the circumstances of the case.

Principle of Law: A person cannot claim compensation merely because they suffer loss when no legal right has been violated.

Difference Between Damnum Sine Injuria and Injuria Sine Damno

1. Damnum Sine Injuria:

• It means actual damage without violation of a legal right.

• No action generally lies in a court of law.

2. Injuria Sine Damno:

• It means violation of a legal right without actual damage.

• A legal action can be brought even if no actual loss is suffered.

Conclusion:

Damnum Sine Injuria is an important principle of tort law which means damage without legal injury. It protects individuals who perform lawful acts from unnecessary legal claims. The cases of Gloucester Grammar School, Mogul Steamship Co. Ltd. and Chasemore v. Richards establish that mere financial loss is not sufficient to create liability in tort. Violation of a legal right is essential for a successful legal action.

2. Injuria Sine Damno (Violation of Legal Right Without Actual Damage)

Introduction:

Injuria Sine Damno is an important legal maxim in the law of torts. It means violation of a legal right without causing actual damage or loss. According to this principle, when the legal right of a person is violated, they can bring a legal action against the wrongdoer even if they have not suffered any actual financial loss or physical injury.

This principle is based on the maxim Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium, which means where there is a right, there is a remedy. It protects the legal rights of individuals.

Meaning of Injuria Sine Damno

• Injuria: Means violation of a legal right.

• Sine: Means without.

• Damno: Means damage or actual loss.

• Therefore, Injuria Sine Damno means violation of a legal right without actual damage.

Essential Features of Injuria Sine Damno

1. Violation of Legal Right:

There must be a violation of a legal right belonging to the plaintiff.

2. No Actual Damage Required:

The plaintiff does not need to prove financial loss, physical injury or any other actual damage.

3. Right to Take Legal Action:

The injured person can file a case in court because their legal right has been violated.

4. Nominal Damages:

The court may award nominal damages even when the plaintiff has not suffered actual financial loss. Nominal damages recognise that a legal right has been violated.

5. Protection of Legal Rights:

This principle ensures that legal rights are protected and that a person cannot violate another person's rights without facing legal consequences.

Important Case Laws
1. Ashby v. White (1703)

Facts: The plaintiff was a legally qualified voter in a parliamentary election. The defendant was a returning officer who wrongfully refused to accept the plaintiff's vote. The candidate for whom the plaintiff wanted to vote was elected even without his vote. Therefore, the plaintiff did not suffer any actual loss in the election.

Judgment: The Court ultimately recognised the plaintiff's right to bring an action against the returning officer, even though no actual damage had been suffered.

Principle of Law: Violation of a legal right is actionable even without proof of actual damage. A person whose legal right is violated is entitled to seek a legal remedy.

2. Bhim Singh v. State of Jammu and Kashmir (1985)

Facts: Bhim Singh was a Member of the Legislative Assembly of Jammu and Kashmir. He was unlawfully arrested and detained by the police while travelling to attend an Assembly session. He was deliberately prevented from attending the session and exercising his rights as a member of the Assembly.

Judgment: The Supreme Court held that his constitutional rights had been violated and directed the State of Jammu and Kashmir to pay him compensation of Rs. 50,000.

Principle of Law: Violation of a person's legal and constitutional rights can justify monetary compensation even when the person has already been released from detention.

Difference Between Injuria Sine Damno and Damnum Sine Injuria

1. Injuria Sine Damno:

• It means violation of a legal right without actual damage.

• A legal action can be brought even without proof of actual loss.

2. Damnum Sine Injuria:

• It means actual damage without violation of a legal right.

• No legal action generally lies merely because financial loss has occurred.

Conclusion:

Injuria Sine Damno is an important principle of tort law which protects the legal rights of individuals. It establishes that actual damage is not necessary when a legal right has been violated. The cases of Ashby v. White (1703) and Bhim Singh v. State of Jammu and Kashmir (1985) clearly explain this principle. Thus, the law provides a remedy for the violation of legal rights even when no actual loss has been suffered.
