Law of Torts & Consumer Protection · Topic 1.3

Characteristics of Tort

The main characteristics of tort explain the essential features of a tortious wrong. These characteristics are as follows:

1. Violation of a Legal Right and Breach of Legal Duty

A tort involves the violation of a person's legal right due to the breach of a legal duty imposed by law.

2. Civil Wrong

Tort is a civil wrong and is different from a criminal wrong. Its main purpose is to provide relief to the injured person. However, the same act may sometimes amount to both a tort and a crime.

3. Right to Claim Unliquidated Damages

The injured person can generally claim unliquidated damages, which means compensation whose amount is determined by the court. Other remedies, such as injunction, may also be available.

4. Independent of Contract

Tortious liability arises from a duty imposed by law and does not depend on any prior agreement between the parties. It is different from a breach of contract.

5. Intention is Not Always Necessary

Intention to cause harm is not essential in every tort. A person may also be held liable for negligence or, in certain cases, without proof of fault.

6. Motive is Generally Irrelevant

The motive behind an act is generally not important in determining tortious liability. However, it may be relevant in certain types of torts.

7. Injury to Person or Property

A tort may involve injury to a person's body, property, reputation or personal liberty.
