1. Introduction

The Law of Torts is concerned with the protection of a variety of interests arising in the society. The cardinal task and the primary object of this branch of law therefore, is the protection of interests by the redistribution of losses among the parties i.e., by requiring the person who invades such an interest to make pecuniary compensation at the suit of the person wronged. Law of Torts is a branch of law which has been developed by judicial process and judicial process is nothing else but evaluation of social events by the judiciary[cite: 26].

A major part of the law of Torts remains still uncodified in England as well as in India[cite: 26]. In India an attempt was made in 1886 in the direction of codifying civil wrongs and Sir Frederick Pollock prepared a bill known as "The Indian Civil Wrongs Bill". But it could not become a law, since then the Government of India showed no inclination to codify this branch of law.

However, there are certain Acts passed by the Indian legislatures from time to time which have embodied certain aspect of the law of torts[cite: 26].

For example, we may mention the following:

  • 1. The Fatal Accidents Act, 1885;
  • 2. The Carriers Act, 1865;
  • 3. The Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923;
  • 4. The Patent and Designs Act, 1911;
  • 5. The Specific Relief Act, 1963;
  • 6. The Sale of Goods Act, 1930;
  • 7. The Indian Easement Act, 1882;
  • 8. The Employers Liability Act, 1938.
2. Definition of Tort

The word 'Tort' has been derived from the Latin term 'tortum' which means 'to twist'. Thus, it implies a conduct which is not lawful but rather it is twisted, crooked or unlawful. The person who commits the tort is called 'tort person' and his misdoing is a 'tortuous act'.

In simple words, tort means a civil wrong committed by one person against another, for which the injured person can claim a legal remedy. There is no single statutory definition of tort in India. However, different jurists have defined it as follows:

1. Definition by Winfield

According to Winfield, “Tortious liability arises from the breach of a duty primarily fixed by law; this duty is towards persons generally, and its breach is redressible by an action for unliquidated damages.”

It means that tort occurs when a person breaches a legal duty imposed by law and causes legal injury to another person.

2. Definition by Salmond

According to Salmond, “A Tort is a civil wrong for which the remedy is a common law action for unliquidated damages and which is not exclusively the breach of a contract or the breach of a trust or other merely equitable obligation.”

It means that the injured person can claim compensation, and the amount is decided by the court.

3. Definition by Fraser

It is an infringement of a right in rem of a private individual giving a right of compensation at the suit of the injured party.

4. Definition by Clerk & Lindsell

A tort may be described as wrong independent of contract, for which the appropriate remedy is common law action.

3. Why It Is Difficult to Define Tort
1. Based on Court Decisions

Tort law is based on decided cases. Judges mainly focus on deciding the cases before them rather than giving general definitions.

2. Complicated Legal Procedures

In England, tort law developed through complicated legal procedures and writs. Therefore, no complete and clear definition was formed.

3. Continuously Developing

Tort law keeps changing with society. Courts recognise new torts by gradually extending existing laws. Therefore, a final and complete definition is difficult to frame.
