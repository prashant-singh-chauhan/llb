Law of Torts & Consumer Protection · Topic 1.4

Constituents of Tort

The constituents of tort are the essential elements that must be present to establish tortious liability. The following are the three main constituents of a tort:

1. Wrongful Act or Omission

There must be a wrongful act or omission by a person. It means that a person must do something which the law prohibits or fail to perform a duty imposed by law. The act must violate a legal duty.

2. Legal Damage

There must be a violation of a legal right of another person. Legal damage does not always mean actual financial loss. Even without actual loss, a person may bring an action if a legal right has been violated.

Example: In Ashby v. White, the plaintiff was wrongfully prevented from voting. Although he suffered no financial loss, his legal right was violated.

3. Legal Remedy

The wrongful act must give rise to a legal remedy. The injured person may approach the court for appropriate relief, such as damages or an injunction.

Legal Maxim: Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium means "Where there is a right, there is a remedy."

Conclusion

Thus, a wrongful act or omission, violation of a legal right and availability of a legal remedy are the three main constituents of tort. These elements help determine whether a person can be held liable under the law of torts.
