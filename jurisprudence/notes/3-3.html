Module 03 · Analytical School

Hans Kelsen

Jurisprudence · Topic 3.2

1. Introduction

Hans Kelsen (1881–1973) was a famous jurist and an important thinker of Legal Positivism. He developed the Pure Theory of Law to explain the nature and validity of law in a systematic and scientific manner. Kelsen wanted jurisprudence to study law as law, without mixing it with morality, religion, politics, sociology or other subjects.

2. Meaning of Pure Theory of Law

Kelsen called his theory “pure” because it deals mainly with the legal system and legal norms. According to him, law is a system of norms which tells people what ought to be done. The validity of a legal rule depends upon its position in the legal system and the authority given to it by a higher legal norm.

3. Main Features of Kelsen’s Theory
(A) Law is a System of Norms

According to Kelsen, law consists of norms. A norm tells a person what ought to be done. Therefore, law is a normative system and not merely a description of what people actually do.

(B) Separation of Law from Morality

Kelsen separated law and morality. According to him, the legal validity of a rule does not depend upon whether it is morally good or bad. Jurisprudence should examine whether a rule is legally valid, while questions of justice and morality are separate matters.

(C) Hierarchy of Norms

The most important feature of Kelsen’s theory is the hierarchy of legal norms. Legal norms are arranged in different levels. A lower norm gets its validity from a higher norm which authorises its creation.

The hierarchy may be shown as:

Grundnorm → Constitution → Acts/Laws → Rules and Regulations → Individual Orders

Thus, a rule made by an authority must be authorised by an Act, and the Act must be made according to the Constitution.

(D) Grundnorm or Basic Norm

The Grundnorm means the Basic Norm. It is placed at the top of Kelsen’s legal system. It is not an ordinary law made by Parliament or any other authority. It is a presupposed norm which provides the ultimate basis for the validity of the legal system.

For examination purposes, the supremacy of the Constitution can be used as an illustration, but the Grundnorm should not simply be treated as identical to the written Constitution.

(E) Dynamic Nature of Law

Kelsen considered law to be a dynamic system. New legal norms are continuously created according to the authority given by higher norms. Therefore, the legal system develops through legally authorised procedures.

(F) Law and State

Kelsen did not consider the State and law as completely separate. The State can be understood through its legal order. Thus, State and law are closely connected.

4. Merits of Kelsen’s Theory

Kelsen’s theory made the study of jurisprudence more systematic and scientific. It clearly explains the relationship between different legal rules and the idea of legal validity.

The concept of hierarchy of norms is also useful in understanding constitutional supremacy, because lower laws must conform to higher legal norms.

5. Criticism of Kelsen’s Theory
1. Grundnorm is Uncertain

The biggest criticism is directed against the Grundnorm. Critics ask: if every legal rule gets validity from a higher rule, from where does the Grundnorm get its validity?

Kelsen says that it is simply presupposed. Critics argue that this does not fully solve the problem of the ultimate source of legal validity.

2. Too Abstract and Theoretical

Kelsen's theory is considered too abstract. It mainly studies the formal structure of law and gives less importance to the actual working of law in society.

Law is also affected by social, economic and political conditions, but Kelsen deliberately keeps these matters outside his “pure” theory.

3. Ignores Social Factors

Law does not work in isolation from society. Customs, social needs, economic conditions and political circumstances influence the development and operation of law. Kelsen's theory gives very little importance to these factors.

4. Ignores Morality and Justice

Kelsen separates law from morality. Therefore, according to his approach, a law may be legally valid even if it is morally wrong.

Critics argue that justice, morality and fairness are important aspects of law and should not be completely ignored.

5. Difficulty in Explaining Changes in Legal Systems

Kelsen's theory faces difficulties when there is a revolution or sudden change of government. If a new government successfully replaces the old constitutional order, the legal system may change, but explaining the new Grundnorm becomes difficult.

6. Not Fully Practical

The theory explains the validity and structure of law, but it does not adequately explain how judges actually decide cases or how laws affect ordinary people.

“Dry Bones of Law” – Criticism

Kelsen’s theory has been criticised as leaving only the “dry bones” of law, without the “flesh and blood” that gives law life.

This criticism means that Kelsen concentrated so much on the formal structure and validity of law that he gave insufficient importance to the actual working of law in society.

1. Ignores Social Factors

Law works within society. Social customs, economic conditions, political circumstances and human behaviour influence law. Kelsen largely keeps these matters outside his Pure Theory.

2. Ignores Morality and Justice

Critics say that justice, fairness and morality are important parts of law. By separating them completely from legal validity, Kelsen gives an incomplete picture of law.

3. Too Abstract

The theory is considered highly theoretical and abstract. It explains the structure of legal norms but does not sufficiently explain how law actually affects ordinary people.

4. Problem of Grundnorm

The idea of Grundnorm is also criticised. If every norm gets validity from a higher norm, critics ask: Where does the Grundnorm itself get its validity?

Kelsen answers that it is presupposed, but critics consider this answer unsatisfactory.

5. Difficult to Explain Changes in Government

Kelsen’s theory also faces difficulties in explaining legal changes after a revolution or successful change of government, because a new legal order may require a new Basic Norm.

Is the “Dry Bones” Criticism Correct?

To some extent, yes. Kelsen gives us a clear explanation of the structure, validity and hierarchy of law, which is a major contribution to jurisprudence.

However, law is not only a system of norms. Law also operates in a real society and is connected with justice, morality, social needs and human behaviour.

Therefore, Kelsen gives us the skeleton or structure of law, but other schools help us understand the life and social working of law.

6. Position of Grundnorm in India

The question of Grundnorm is particularly important in India. India has a written Constitution, and the Constitution is the supreme legal authority. Parliament, State Legislatures, Executive and Judiciary all derive their powers from the Constitution. Therefore, in a simple examination sense, the Indian Constitution can be treated as the highest or fundamental legal norm in the Indian legal system. However, technically, Kelsen’s Grundnorm should not be simply equated with the written Constitution. The Grundnorm is a presupposed basic norm, whereas the Constitution is an actual legal document.

7. Conclusion

Kelsen’s Pure Theory of Law is an important theory of Legal Positivism. Its main principles are law as a system of norms, separation of law and morality, hierarchy of norms, legal validity and Grundnorm. Although the theory has certain limitations, it provides a clear and systematic explanation of the structure and validity of a legal system.
