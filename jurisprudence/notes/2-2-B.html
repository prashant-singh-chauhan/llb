Module 02 · Natural Law School

Kohler

Jurisprudence · Topic 2.2 B

1. Introduction and Background

Joseph (Josef) Kohler (1849–1919) was a German jurist and an important thinker associated with the revival of Natural Law in the modern period. He was influenced by the philosophy of Hegel and is generally associated with the Neo-Hegelian school of thought.

Kohler believed that law is closely connected with the cultural development of society. According to him, law should not remain fixed forever because society, civilisation, and human needs continue to change. His theory gives importance to culture, social progress, and the changing nature of law.

Unlike traditional Natural Law thinkers who believed in permanent and unchanging legal principles, Kohler emphasised that legal principles must develop according to the culture and conditions of each society.

2. Definition of Law According to Kohler

Kohler defined law as a standard of conduct which arises from the collective life of society and is imposed upon individuals to guide their behaviour towards a reasonable form of life.

In simple words, law represents the collective needs and purposes of society. It regulates individual conduct and helps maintain social order and cultural development.

Thus, Kohler considered law a means of organising society and promoting cultural progress.

3. Main Principle of Kohler's Theory

The main principle of Kohler's theory is that law must change according to the cultural development of society.

He rejected the idea that one fixed system of law could be suitable for every society and every period. Different societies have different cultures, needs, customs, and levels of development. Therefore, their legal systems should also differ.

According to Kohler, the purpose of law is to support and promote the cultural development of humanity. When society progresses, the law must also develop to meet its changing needs.

4. Law and Culture

The relationship between law and culture is the most important part of Kohler's theory. He believed that law is an instrument for promoting and advancing culture.

Meaning of Culture: Culture refers to the achievements of humanity in different fields, including social life, education, science, art, morality, and civilisation.

Role of Law: Law provides rules and institutions that help society develop its cultural values, maintain social order, and regulate human relationships.

Changing Culture: As culture develops, people's needs and social relationships change. Therefore, laws must also change to remain suitable for society.

Example: The laws required in an agricultural society may differ from those needed in a modern industrial or digital society. As society develops, laws relating to employment, property, business, and technology may need to change.

Thus, according to Kohler, law should support the changing cultural needs of society rather than remain permanently fixed.

5. Rejection of Eternal and Unchanging Natural Law

Kohler did not accept the traditional idea of a fixed and eternal Natural Law that remains suitable for every age and civilisation.

He argued that human civilisation is constantly progressing. A legal rule suitable for one period may become unsuitable for another period because social conditions and cultural values change.

Therefore, Kohler believed that each culture should have a legal system suited to its own conditions and stage of development.

His approach made Natural Law more flexible and relative to the development of society. It moved away from the idea that the same detailed legal rules should apply everywhere and at all times.

6. Law as an Instrument of Cultural Progress

Kohler regarded law as a means of promoting and protecting the cultural achievements of humanity. Law should not merely maintain order; it should also help society progress.

According to him, a legal system should respond to the changing needs of people and encourage the development of social institutions, knowledge, and civilisation.

Therefore, laws should be revised whenever necessary to keep pace with cultural advancement. A law that no longer serves the needs of society may require reform.

7. Individual and Society

Kohler recognised the importance of both individual freedom and collective welfare. He believed that individual development is necessary for social progress, but society also requires cooperation and common rules.

Law should maintain a proper balance between individual interests and the interests of the community. It should allow individuals to develop freely while preserving social harmony and cooperation.

Thus, Kohler's theory attempts to reconcile individual freedom with the collective needs of society.

8. Jural Postulates and Kohler's Contribution

Kohler's emphasis on the legal needs of particular societies is connected with the idea of jural postulates, meaning the basic legal expectations and assumptions that a society needs its legal system to protect.

Such expectations may change according to time, place, and cultural conditions. Laws and legal institutions should therefore be developed to respond to the needs and expectations of the society in which they operate.

American jurist Roscoe Pound recognised the importance of developing legal postulates suited to the conditions of a particular time and place. Kohler's cultural approach helped strengthen the view that law must be understood in relation to the changing needs of society.

9. Importance of Kohler's Theory

1. Flexibility of Law: Kohler explained that law must change according to the changing conditions of society.

2. Cultural Development: He emphasised that law should promote and protect the cultural achievements of humanity.

3. Rejection of Fixed Rules: He challenged the idea that one permanent system of legal rules could suit every society and every period.

4. Social Progress: He considered law an important instrument for promoting social development and civilisation.

5. Balance of Interests: He recognised the importance of balancing individual freedom with the welfare of the community.

6. Influence on Modern Jurisprudence: His approach contributed to the understanding that legal systems must be studied in relation to culture, social conditions, and changing human needs.

10. Criticism of Kohler's Theory

1. Excessive Emphasis on Culture: Critics argue that Kohler gives too much importance to cultural development and may not give sufficient weight to universal moral principles.

2. Uncertainty: If law changes according to culture, it may become difficult to identify principles of justice that apply to every society.

3. Possibility of Injustice: A cultural practice may itself be unjust. Therefore, culture alone cannot always be the standard for deciding whether a law is fair.

4. Lack of a Fixed Standard: Kohler's theory does not provide a single, permanent set of legal rules that can be applied to every society.

5. Difficulty in Application: It may be difficult to determine which cultural values should guide law when different groups within the same society have different beliefs and interests.

Despite these criticisms, Kohler's theory remains important because it highlights the relationship between law, culture, and social progress.

11. Conclusion

Joseph Kohler made an important contribution to modern legal thought by emphasising the relationship between law and culture. He rejected the idea of a fixed legal system suitable for every society and every period.

According to Kohler, law should change with cultural development and meet the changing needs of society. He considered law an instrument for promoting civilisation, social progress, and the welfare of the community.

Therefore, the central idea of Kohler's theory is that law must adapt to the cultural development of society rather than remain fixed and unchanging.

Easy way to remember: Kohler = Culture + Changing Law + Social Progress + Individual and Community Interests.
