Module 02 · Natural Law School

Rudolf Stammler

Jurisprudence · Topic 2.2 A

Natural Law with Variable Content

1. Meaning and Background

Rudolf Stammler (1856–1938) was a German jurist and an important thinker in the revival of Natural Law. He was influenced by the philosophy of Immanuel Kant. His main aim was to find a standard of justice that could be applied to different societies and different periods.

Stammler called his theory "Natural Law with Variable Content." According to him, the principles of justice provide a standard for judging law, but the actual content of law may change according to the needs and conditions of society.

2. Stammler's Concept of Law

Stammler described law as a form of will that is external, self-authoritative, and inviolable and is intended to regulate human conduct in society. Law helps organise social life and the relationships between individuals.

He made an important distinction between the Concept of Law and the Idea of Law.

1. Concept of Law: It explains what law is as a form of binding social regulation. It concerns the nature and characteristics of law.

2. Idea of Law: It explains what law should be in order to achieve justice. It provides a standard for judging whether existing laws are just or unjust.

Thus, the concept of law explains the nature of law, while the idea of law provides a standard for judging its justice.

3. Natural Law with Variable Content

This is the most important part of Stammler's theory. He believed that the principles of justice provide a standard for evaluating law, but the specific rules of law cannot remain the same in every society and at every time.

The actual content of law changes according to time, place, culture, and social conditions. However, the aim should remain the achievement of justice and respect for human freedom.

Example: The laws relating to property, labour, family, and business may differ between an agricultural society and an industrial society. The rules may change according to social needs, but they should aim at justice.

Therefore, Stammler called his theory Natural Law with Variable Content, because the content of law changes while the principles of justice provide a standard for evaluating it.

4. Community of Free-Willing Persons

According to Stammler, the ideal society is a "community of free-willing persons." He believed that every individual should be treated as a free and reasonable human being.

Individual freedom should not be used to destroy the equal freedom of others. The purpose of law is to harmonise the different purposes and interests of people living in society.

Thus, a just legal system should respect individual freedom, equality, mutual respect, and social cooperation.

5. Principles of Just Law

Stammler laid down four principles of justice, which are divided into two groups: Principles of Respect and Principles of Participation.

A. Principles of Respect

1. No Arbitrary Control: The will of one person should not be made subject to the arbitrary will of another person.

2. Respect for Every Person: Every person affected by a legal rule should be treated as a fellow human being and not merely as a means to achieve another person's purpose.

B. Principles of Participation

3. No Arbitrary Exclusion: No member of the legal community should be arbitrarily excluded from membership of that community.

4. Respect in Exercising Legal Power: Legal powers should be exercised in a way that respects the affected person's value and freedom as a human being.

These four principles provide a formal standard of justice. They do not prescribe one fixed set of legal rules for every society.

6. Importance of Stammler's Theory

1. Revival of Natural Law: Stammler helped revive Natural Law in modern jurisprudence without simply returning to its older religious form.

2. Universal Standard of Justice: He provided principles of justice that could be used to evaluate the fairness of laws.

3. Changing Social Conditions: He recognised that laws must change according to the needs and circumstances of society.

4. Human Freedom and Equality: He emphasised respect for individual freedom, equality, and human dignity.

5. Relationship Between Law and Justice: He explained that the existence of a law and the justice of that law are two different questions.

7. Criticism of Stammler's Theory

1. Abstract Principles: His principles of justice are general and may be difficult to apply to particular legal problems.

2. Lack of Clear Answers: The four principles do not always provide a definite answer about which legal rule should be adopted.

3. Uncertainty: Critics question how Natural Law can remain a universal standard when the actual content of law changes from society to society.

4. Difficulty in Application: Different people may interpret freedom, equality, and justice differently, making the practical application of his theory difficult.

Despite these criticisms, Stammler's theory remains important because it connects law with justice while recognising changing social conditions.

8. Conclusion

Rudolf Stammler made an important contribution to the revival of Natural Law. His central idea was that justice provides a standard for judging law, while the actual content of law changes according to the needs of society.

His theory, known as Natural Law with Variable Content, combines the universal aim of justice with the changing requirements of social life. It emphasises human freedom, equality, mutual respect, and social cooperation.

Easy way to remember: Justice + Free-Willing Persons + Four Principles of Justice + Variable Content.
