Jurisprudence · Topic 3.1

1. Introduction

The Analytical School of Jurisprudence, also known as the Positive or Imperative School, studies law as it exists rather than what law ought to be.

It examines the nature, sources, and basic concepts of positive law recognised by the State.

Jeremy Bentham and John Austin were its leading thinkers. Later jurists, such as H.L.A. Hart and Hans Kelsen, also developed important positivist theories. This school focuses on the legal rules and their validity rather than their morality.

2. Salient Features of Analytical School
1. Law as It Is

The Analytical School studies law as it actually exists in a legal system. It does not primarily judge laws on the basis of morality or fairness. A law may be morally unjust but still legally valid if it satisfies the requirements of the legal system. This approach is known as studying law as it is.

2. Positive Law Is the Main Subject

According to the Analytical School, jurisprudence mainly deals with positive law, which means law established or recognised by a legal system. It includes legislation, legal rules, and other rules recognised as law by the relevant legal system.

The school aims to understand the nature and structure of existing laws rather than simply discussing what the law should be.

3. Separation of Law and Morality

The Analytical School distinguishes between legal validity and moral value. A law may be legally valid even if it is morally wrong. Therefore, the existence of a law and its moral worth are two separate questions.

For example, an unjust tax law may still be legally valid if it has been properly enacted. However, this does not mean that the law is morally right or should always be obeyed.

4. Importance of Sovereignty

John Austin gave great importance to sovereignty. According to his command theory, law is a command of the sovereign backed by a sanction.

The sovereign is a person or body that receives habitual obedience from the majority of the population and does not habitually obey another earthly authority.

His theory can be remembered through the following formula:

Sovereign → Command → Duty → Sanction

Thus, according to Austin, a legal command imposes a duty, and disobedience is backed by the threat of punishment. This is specifically Austin's theory and is not accepted in the same form by every analytical jurist.

5. Analytical Method

The school uses the analytical method to study legal concepts by examining their basic elements and meanings. It analyses concepts such as rights, duties, ownership, possession, liability, sovereignty, and sanctions.

This method helps make the study of law more systematic, clear, and precise.

6. Importance of State Authority and Legal Rules

The Analytical School gives importance to laws established or recognised by competent legal authorities. Legislation plays an important role in creating and modifying legal rules.

However, not every analytical jurist believes that all law must be a direct command of the State. For example, Austin's theory explains custom differently from later positivist theories.

7. Limited Emphasis on Historical Development

The Analytical School mainly focuses on the structure and meaning of existing law rather than tracing its historical origin and development.

This distinguishes it from the Historical School, which gives greater importance to customs, traditions, and the historical growth of law.

Critical Analysis

The Analytical School has several merits. It brings clarity, certainty, and order to the study of law. It helps explain legal concepts such as rights, duties, sovereignty, and liability.

However, the classical theory also has limitations. It gives considerable importance to State authority and does not adequately explain every form of customary, constitutional, and international law. Austin's command theory also struggles to explain laws that confer legal powers, such as those governing contracts and wills, because these laws do not merely impose duties backed by punishment. These limitations were among the issues addressed by later positivists, particularly H.L.A. Hart.

Conclusion

The Analytical School is an important school of jurisprudence. Its main features include the study of law as it exists, the importance of positive law, the distinction between law and morality, Austin's theory of sovereignty, the analytical method, and the study of existing legal rules.

Although the school provides a systematic understanding of law, it cannot fully explain every aspect of legal systems through Austin's command theory alone. Therefore, it should be studied alongside other schools of jurisprudence.
