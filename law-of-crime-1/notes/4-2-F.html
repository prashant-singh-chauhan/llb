Criminal Breach of Trust

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 4.2 F

Criminal Breach of Trust
1. Introduction

Criminal breach of trust is an offence against property. It occurs when a person is entrusted with property or control over property and dishonestly uses that property for personal benefit or deals with it against the law or the terms of an agreement. The offence is defined under Section 316 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS). It protects people who hand over their property to another person for a particular purpose.

2. Definition under Section 316(1) BNS

According to Section 316(1), whoever, being in any manner entrusted with property, or with any dominion over property, dishonestly misappropriates or converts it to his own use, or dishonestly uses or disposes of it in violation of any direction of law or any legal contract, express or implied, relating to the discharge of that trust, or wilfully allows another person to do so, commits criminal breach of trust.

Explanation in Simple Words: If a person receives property for a particular purpose but dishonestly uses it for personal benefit or against the agreed purpose, that person may commit criminal breach of trust.

3. Essential Ingredients of Criminal Breach of Trust

1. Entrustment of Property: The accused must have been entrusted with property or given control over it. For example, a person may entrust money to an agent to purchase goods on their behalf.

2. Dishonest Misappropriation or Conversion: The accused must dishonestly use the property as their own or convert it for personal benefit. For example, an agent who uses the entrusted money for personal expenses instead of purchasing the agreed goods may commit the offence.

3. Violation of Law or Contract: The accused must dishonestly use or dispose of the property contrary to a legal direction or an express or implied contract governing its use.

4. Dishonest Intention: The accused must act dishonestly. Mere failure to perform a contract or make a payment does not automatically amount to criminal breach of trust. The required dishonest conduct and other legal elements must be established.

5. Wilfully Allowing Another Person to Misuse the Property: The offence also covers a person who wilfully allows another person to misappropriate or misuse the entrusted property in the prohibited manner.

4. Explanations under Section 316

Explanation 1 — Provident Fund Contributions: An employer who deducts an employee's contribution from wages for payment into a Provident Fund or Family Pension Fund is deemed to have been entrusted with that money. If the employer fails to pay the contribution into the fund in violation of the applicable law, the employer is deemed to have dishonestly used the money in violation of a legal direction.

Explanation 2 — Employees' State Insurance Contributions: Similarly, an employer who deducts employees' contributions from wages for payment into the Employees' State Insurance Fund is deemed to have been entrusted with that money. Failure to pay the contribution into the fund in violation of the applicable law is deemed to be dishonest use contrary to a legal direction.

5. Statutory Illustrations under Section 316(1)

Illustration (a): Executor of a Will

A, being executor to the will of a deceased person, dishonestly disobeys the law which directs him to divide the effects according to the will, and appropriates them to his own use. A has committed criminal breach of trust.

Explanation: An executor must distribute the deceased person's property according to the will and applicable law. Dishonestly taking that property for personal use amounts to criminal breach of trust.

Illustration (b): Warehouse-Keeper

A is a warehouse-keeper. Z, going on a journey, entrusts his furniture to A, under a contract that it shall be returned on payment of a stipulated sum for warehouse room. A dishonestly sells the goods. A has committed criminal breach of trust.

Explanation: A receives the furniture for safe keeping, not for sale. Dishonestly selling the entrusted furniture violates the terms of the arrangement.

Illustration (c): Agent Misusing Money

A, residing in Kolkata, is agent for Z, residing at Delhi. There is an express or implied contract between A and Z, that all sums remitted by Z to A shall be invested by A, according to Z's direction. Z remits one lakh of rupees to A, with directions to A to invest the same in Company's paper. A dishonestly disobeys the directions and employs the money in his own business. A has committed criminal breach of trust.

Explanation: A was entrusted with money for a specific investment. Using that money dishonestly in his own business amounts to criminal breach of trust.

Illustration (d): Acting in Good Faith

But if A, in illustration (c), not dishonestly but in good faith, believing that it will be more for Z's advantage to hold shares in the Bank of Bengal, disobeys Z's directions, and buys shares in the Bank of Bengal, for Z, instead of buying Company's paper, here, though Z should suffer loss, and should be entitled to bring a civil action against A, on account of that loss, yet A, not having acted dishonestly, has not committed criminal breach of trust.

Explanation: A acts honestly and believes that the alternative investment will benefit Z. Even if Z suffers a loss, criminal breach of trust is not established because dishonest intention is absent. A may still face civil liability.

Illustration (e): Revenue Officer

A, a revenue-officer, is entrusted with public money and is either directed by law, or bound by a contract, express or implied, with the Government, to pay into a certain treasury all the public money which he holds. A dishonestly appropriates the money. A has committed criminal breach of trust.

Explanation: A revenue officer must deposit public money in the prescribed treasury. Dishonestly taking that money for personal use violates the legal duty attached to the entrustment.

Illustration (f): Carrier of Goods

A, a carrier, is entrusted by Z with property to be carried by land or by water. A dishonestly misappropriates the property. A has committed criminal breach of trust.

Explanation: A carrier receives property for transportation. Dishonestly keeping or using that property instead of delivering it to the intended destination amounts to criminal breach of trust.

6. Punishment under Section 316 BNS

Section 316(2) — Ordinary Criminal Breach of Trust: Imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to five years, or fine, or both.

Section 316(3) — Carrier, Wharfinger or Warehouse-Keeper: Imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven years, and liability to fine.

Section 316(4) — Clerk or Servant: Imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven years, and liability to fine, where the property was entrusted to the person in that capacity.

Section 316(5) — Public Servant, Banker, Merchant, Factor, Broker, Attorney or Agent: Imprisonment for life, or imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to ten years, and liability to fine, where the property was entrusted in the capacity or business specified in this subsection.

7. Conclusion

Criminal breach of trust arises when a person entrusted with property dishonestly misuses, converts, disposes of or allows the misuse of that property contrary to law or the terms of the trust. The most important requirements are entrustment and dishonest conduct. Section 316 of the BNS provides different punishments depending on the position of the accused and the nature of the entrustment. Its illustrations explain that dishonest misuse is punishable, whereas an honest decision made in good faith does not automatically constitute criminal breach of trust.
