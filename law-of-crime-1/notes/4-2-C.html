Module 04 · Specific Offences

Robbery

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 4.2 C

Robbery under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023
1. Introduction

Robbery is a serious offence against property. It involves theft or extortion accompanied by violence, wrongful restraint, or fear of immediate harm in the circumstances specified by law. Robbery is defined under Section 309 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS). Every robbery involves either theft or extortion.

2. Meaning and Definition of Robbery

Under Section 309(1), every robbery involves either theft or extortion. According to Section 309(2), theft amounts to robbery when the offender voluntarily causes or attempts to cause death, hurt, or wrongful restraint, or fear of instant death, instant hurt, or instant wrongful restraint, in order to commit theft, while committing theft, or while carrying away or attempting to carry away stolen property.

Under Section 309(3), extortion amounts to robbery when the offender is present, puts the victim in fear of instant death, instant hurt, or instant wrongful restraint to that person or another person, and thereby induces the victim to deliver the property immediately.

3. Essential Elements of Robbery

1. Theft or Extortion: Robbery must involve either theft or extortion.

2. Violence or Immediate Fear in Theft: In connection with committing theft or carrying away stolen property, the offender must voluntarily cause or attempt to cause death, hurt, or wrongful restraint, or fear of instant death, instant hurt, or instant wrongful restraint.

3. Presence of the Offender in Extortion: The offender must be sufficiently near the victim to create fear of immediate harm.

4. Fear of Immediate Harm: In extortion, the victim must be put in fear of instant death, instant hurt, or instant wrongful restraint to that person or another person.

5. Immediate Delivery of Property: Because of that fear, the victim must deliver the property then and there.

3. Illustrations

Example 1: A holds B down and takes money from B's pocket. A commits robbery because he uses wrongful restraint to commit theft.

4. Illustrations of Robbery

Illustration 1: Robbery by Theft

A holds Z down and takes money and jewellery from Z's clothes without Z's consent. A has committed theft by using wrongful restraint and, therefore, commits robbery.

Illustration 2: Robbery by Extortion

A meets Z on a road, shows a pistol, and demands Z's purse. Z gives the purse because of fear of immediate hurt. A commits robbery by extortion.

Illustration 3: Threat to a Child

A takes Z's child and threatens to throw the child down a precipice unless Z gives him a purse. Z delivers the purse because of fear of immediate harm to the child. A commits robbery by extortion.

Illustration 4: Threat of Future Harm

A tells Z that his child is held by A's gang and will be killed unless Z sends ₹10,000. This amounts to extortion, but it does not amount to robbery unless Z is put in fear of the child's instant death.

5. Punishment for Robbery

Under Section 309(4), robbery is punishable with rigorous imprisonment for up to ten years and fine. If robbery is committed on a highway between sunset and sunrise, imprisonment may extend to fourteen years.

Under Section 309(5), an attempt to commit robbery is punishable with rigorous imprisonment for up to seven years and fine.

Under Section 309(6), if a person voluntarily causes hurt while committing or attempting robbery, that person and any other person jointly concerned in the offence may be punished with imprisonment for life, or rigorous imprisonment for up to ten years, and fine.

6. Difference Between Theft and Robbery

Theft involves dishonestly taking movable property from another person's possession without consent. Robbery is a more serious form of theft or extortion because it involves the additional elements of violence, wrongful restraint, or fear of immediate harm prescribed by law. Therefore, every robbery involves theft or extortion, but every theft or extortion does not amount to robbery.

4. When Does Robbery Amount to Dacoity?

Under Section 310 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, robbery amounts to dacoity when five or more persons conjointly commit or attempt to commit robbery, or when the total number of persons conjointly committing or attempting robbery, together with those present and aiding them, is five or more.

Example: Five persons surround a shopkeeper, threaten to kill him immediately, and force him to hand over cash. If the legal requirements of robbery are fulfilled, their joint act amounts to dacoity.

7. Conclusion

Robbery is an aggravated form of theft or extortion under Section 309 of the BNS. The main factors distinguishing robbery are the use of violence or wrongful restraint, fear of immediate harm, the offender's presence in cases of extortion, and immediate delivery of property. The law provides severe punishment to protect people and their property from such offences.
