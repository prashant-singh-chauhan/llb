Module 04 · Specific Offences

Cheating

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 4.2 G

Cheating under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)
1. Introduction

Cheating is an offence against property and other personal interests. It occurs when a person deceives another person and dishonestly or fraudulently induces that person to deliver property or to do or omit something that causes, or is likely to cause, harm. The offence of cheating is defined under Section 318 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS). Cheating by personation is separately covered under Section 319.

2. Definition of Cheating under Section 318(1)

According to Section 318(1) of the BNS, whoever, by deceiving any person, fraudulently or dishonestly induces the person so deceived to deliver any property to any person, or to consent that any person shall retain any property, or intentionally induces the person so deceived to do or omit to do anything which he would not do or omit if he were not so deceived, and which act or omission causes or is likely to cause damage or harm to that person in body, mind, reputation or property, is said to cheat.

Explanation under the Bare Act: A dishonest concealment of facts is a deception within the meaning of this section.

Meaning in Simple Words: Cheating occurs when a person intentionally deceives another person and uses that deception to obtain property or make that person act, or refrain from acting, in a way that causes or may cause harm.

3. Essential Ingredients of Cheating

1. Deception: The accused must deceive another person through a false statement, false representation or dishonest concealment of facts.

2. Fraudulent or Dishonest Inducement: The deception must induce the victim to deliver property, allow someone to retain property, or take some other action or omission covered by the section.

3. Damage or Harm: The induced act or omission must cause, or be likely to cause, harm to the victim's body, mind, reputation or property.

4. Dishonest Intention: The accused must possess the necessary dishonest or fraudulent intention. In cases involving a promise, the intention to deceive must ordinarily exist when the promise is made. Mere failure to fulfil a genuine promise does not automatically amount to cheating.

4. Statutory Illustrations under Section 318(1)

Illustration (a): False Claim of Government Employment

A, by falsely pretending to be in the Civil Service, intentionally deceives Z, and thus dishonestly induces Z to let him have on credit goods for which he does not mean to pay. A cheats.

Explanation: A falsely claims to hold a government position to obtain goods on credit without intending to pay. His dishonest deception amounts to cheating.

Illustration (b): Counterfeit Mark on Goods

A, by putting a counterfeit mark on an article, intentionally deceives Z into a belief that this article was made by a certain celebrated manufacturer, and thus dishonestly induces Z to buy and pay for the article. A cheats.

Explanation: A uses a false mark to make an article appear to have been manufactured by a famous producer. Z buys it because of the deception.

Illustration (c): False Sample of Goods

A, by exhibiting to Z a false sample of an article intentionally deceives Z into believing that the article corresponds with the sample, and thereby dishonestly induces Z to buy and pay for the article. A cheats.

Explanation: A displays a false sample to mislead Z about the actual quality or nature of the goods. Z pays because of the deception.

Illustration (d): Dishonoured Bill

A, by tendering in payment for an article a bill on a house with which A keeps no money, and by which A expects that the bill will be dishonoured, intentionally deceives Z, and thereby dishonestly induces Z to deliver the article, intending not to pay for it. A cheats.

Explanation: A uses a bill that he expects will not be honoured to obtain goods without intending to pay for them.

Illustration (e): False Representation of Diamonds

A, by pledging as diamonds articles which he knows are not diamonds, intentionally deceives Z, and thereby dishonestly induces Z to lend money. A cheats.

Explanation: A falsely presents ordinary articles as diamonds to obtain a loan from Z.

Illustration (f): False Promise to Repay a Loan

A intentionally deceives Z into a belief that A means to repay any money that Z may lend to him and thereby dishonestly induces Z to lend him money, A not intending to repay it. A cheats.

Explanation: A obtains a loan by falsely claiming that he intends to repay it, when he has no such intention at the time.

Illustration (g): False Promise to Deliver Indigo Plant

A intentionally deceives Z into a belief that A means to deliver to Z a certain quantity of indigo plant which he does not intend to deliver, and thereby dishonestly induces Z to advance money upon the faith of such delivery. A cheats; but if A, at the time of obtaining the money, intends to deliver the indigo plant, and afterwards breaks his contract and does not deliver it, he does not cheat, but is liable only to a civil action for breach of contract.

Explanation: The important difference is the intention at the time of obtaining the money. A dishonest intention from the beginning may amount to cheating, whereas a genuine intention followed by a later breach of contract does not, by itself, amount to cheating.

Illustration (h): False Claim of Performing a Contract

A intentionally deceives Z into a belief that A has performed A's part of a contract made with Z, which he has not performed, and thereby dishonestly induces Z to pay money. A cheats.

Explanation: A falsely claims to have fulfilled a contractual obligation to obtain payment from Z.

Illustration (i): Selling Property Already Sold

A sells and conveys an estate to B. A, knowing that in consequence of such sale he has no right to the property, sells or mortgages the same to Z, without disclosing the fact of the previous sale and conveyance to B, and receives the purchase or mortgage money from Z. A cheats.

Explanation: A has already transferred the property to B but conceals this fact and obtains money from Z by selling or mortgaging it again.

5. Punishment for Cheating under Section 318

Section 318(2) — Ordinary Cheating: Imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years, or fine, or both.

Section 318(3) — Cheating a Person Whose Interests Must Be Protected: If the offender cheats knowing that wrongful loss is likely to be caused to a person whose interests the offender is bound by law or legal contract to protect, the punishment may extend to five years' imprisonment, or fine, or both.

Section 318(4) — Cheating and Dishonestly Inducing Delivery of Property: If the offender cheats and dishonestly induces the victim to deliver property, or to make, alter or destroy a valuable security or a signed or sealed document capable of being converted into a valuable security, the punishment may extend to seven years' imprisonment, and the offender is also liable to fine.

6. Cheating by Personation under Section 319

Section 319 deals with cheating by personation. A person commits this offence by cheating while pretending to be another person, knowingly substituting one person for another, or representing that the person or someone else is a person different from their actual identity.

Explanation: The person being impersonated may be a real person or an imaginary person.

Statutory Illustration (a): A cheats by pretending to be a certain rich banker of the same name. A cheats by personation.

Statutory Illustration (b): A cheats by pretending to be B, a person who is deceased. A cheats by personation.

Punishment: Under Section 319(2), cheating by personation is punishable with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to five years, or fine, or both.

7. Difference Between Cheating and Breach of Contract

Cheating involves deception and dishonest or fraudulent inducement. A breach of contract occurs when a party fails to fulfil a contractual obligation. If a person enters into an agreement honestly but later fails to perform it, that failure does not automatically amount to cheating. However, if the person uses a false promise to obtain money or property while already intending not to fulfil the promise, cheating may be established.

8. Conclusion

Cheating under Section 318 of the BNS involves deception that dishonestly or fraudulently induces a person to deliver property or take an action or omission that causes, or is likely to cause, harm. The statutory illustrations explain different forms of deception, including false claims, counterfeit marks, false samples and dishonest promises. Sections 318 and 319 provide punishment for cheating and cheating by personation, respectively, to protect individuals against fraudulent conduct.
