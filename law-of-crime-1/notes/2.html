Law of Crimes – I · Topic 2.1

Inchoate Crimes / Preliminary Offences

Inchoate crimes, also known as preliminary offences or anticipatory crimes, are offences in which a person may be held criminally liable even though the main intended crime has not been completed. These offences allow the law to prevent criminal activities before the final offence takes place.

1. Abetment: It means instigating, engaging in a relevant conspiracy, or intentionally aiding another person to commit an act. The main offence need not always be completed for abetment to be punishable. It is covered under Sections 45–60 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS).

2. Criminal Conspiracy: It means an agreement between two or more persons to commit an illegal act or to achieve a legal objective by illegal means. It is covered under Section 61 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS).

3. Attempt: It occurs when a person goes beyond preparation and takes an act towards committing an offence, but the offence remains incomplete. The general provision is Section 62 BNS, where no specific punishment for the attempt is provided.
