Module 01 · General Principles of Crime

Common Object

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 1.7

Introduction:

Common object means a common purpose shared by members of a group to achieve a particular objective. In criminal law, it is an important principle for determining the constructive liability of members of an unlawful assembly.

Legal Provision:

Section 190 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) provides that when a member of an unlawful assembly commits an offence in pursuit of its common object, or commits an offence that the members knew was likely to be committed, every member of that assembly may be held liable. An unlawful assembly consists of five or more persons meeting the requirements of Section 189 BNS.

Essential Elements:

1. Five or More Persons: The group must consist of at least five persons and satisfy the legal requirements of an unlawful assembly.

2. Common Object: Members must share an unlawful purpose recognised by law.

3. Commission of an Offence: An offence must be committed in pursuit of the common object or be one the members knew was likely to be committed.

Major Judicial Case:
1. Lalji v. State of Uttar Pradesh (1989)

Facts: Several accused persons were prosecuted for offences committed by members of an unlawful assembly. They challenged their convictions, including murder with the aid of Section 149 IPC, on the ground that individual criminal acts had not been established against every accused.

Judgment: The Supreme Court explained that Section 149 IPC creates constructive or vicarious criminal liability for members of an unlawful assembly when its legal requirements are satisfied.

Principle of Judgment: A member may be held liable for an offence committed in prosecution of the assembly's common object, or for an offence the members knew was likely to be committed in pursuing that object. Proof of a separate overt act by every member is not necessary, but membership and the required common object or knowledge must be established.

Example:

If five or more people form an unlawful assembly to attack a person, members may be held liable for an offence committed in pursuit of their common object, subject to the legal requirements.

Conclusion:

Thus, common object establishes joint criminal liability among members of an unlawful assembly when the requirements of law are fulfilled. Mere association with a group is not sufficient without the necessary legal conditions.
