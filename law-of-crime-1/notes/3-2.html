Module 03 · General Exceptions

Accident

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 3.2

Introduction:

Accident is an important general exception under criminal law. Sometimes, a person causes harm to another person without any criminal intention or knowledge while performing a lawful act carefully. In such circumstances, the person may be protected from criminal liability under Section 18 of the BNS, 2023.

Meaning of Section 18 BNS:

Section 18 provides that an act is not an offence when it occurs by accident or misfortune, without criminal intention or knowledge, while performing a lawful act in a lawful manner, by lawful means, and with proper care and caution.

Essential Elements of Accident as a Defence:

1. Accident or Misfortune: The harm must occur unexpectedly and unintentionally. A deliberate act cannot be treated as an accident merely because its consequences were unexpected.

2. Absence of Criminal Intention or Knowledge: The person must not have the criminal intention or knowledge required for the offence. The harm must result from an accidental occurrence.

3. Lawful Act: The person must be performing an act that is lawful. This defence is generally not available when the harm occurs while committing an unlawful act.

4. Lawful Manner and Lawful Means: The act must be performed in a lawful way and by lawful methods.

5. Proper Care and Caution: The person must exercise reasonable care and caution. If the harm results from a failure to take proper care, the defence may not apply.

Illustration:

Section 18 gives the example of A working with a hatchet. Its head suddenly flies off and kills a person standing nearby. If A was working carefully and there was no lack of proper caution, the death is treated as accidental, and A's act is excusable under this provision.

Important Case Law: Tunda v. Rex (1949)

Facts: Tunda and Munshi were friends who enjoyed wrestling. During a wrestling match, Munshi sustained a head injury when his head struck a hard surface, resulting in his death. Tunda was accused of causing his death through a rash or negligent act.

Judgment: The Allahabad High Court held that the injury was accidental and that there was no sufficient evidence of foul play or a rash act. The Court set aside Tunda's conviction.

Principle of Judgment: The Court recognised that an injury or death occurring accidentally during a lawful activity may be excused when there is no criminal intention or negligence and the required care has been exercised. The case applied Section 80 IPC, which corresponds to Section 18 BNS.

Conclusion:

Thus, Section 18 BNS protects a person from criminal liability when harm occurs by genuine accident during a lawful act performed lawfully and with proper care and caution. However, mere absence of intention is not sufficient; all the conditions of this general exception must be fulfilled.
