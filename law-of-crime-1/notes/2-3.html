Module 02 · Inchoate Crime / Preliminary Offences

Attempt

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 2.3

Introduction:

Attempt is the third stage of crime, following intention and preparation. It occurs when a person, with the intention to commit an offence, takes a direct step towards its commission but fails to complete the offence. Attempt is a punishable stage of criminal conduct under the law.

Meaning of Attempt:

An attempt means doing an act towards committing an offence after making the necessary preparation. The offence remains incomplete because of failure, interruption, or circumstances beyond the person's control. Mere intention or preparation is generally not sufficient to constitute an attempt.

Legal Provision – Section 62 BNS:

Section 62 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, provides the general punishment for attempting to commit offences punishable with imprisonment for life or other imprisonment, where the BNS does not expressly provide a separate punishment for the attempt.

Essential Elements of Attempt:

1. Intention: The person must intend to commit a particular offence.

2. Preparation: The person must have made the preparations necessary for carrying out the intended offence.

3. Direct Act: The person must perform an act towards the commission of the offence. The act must go beyond mere preparation.

4. Incomplete Offence: The intended offence must remain incomplete, although the person has taken a sufficient step towards committing it.

Difference between Preparation and Attempt:

Preparation means arranging the necessary things to commit an offence. Attempt begins when the person takes a sufficiently direct step towards carrying it out. For example, purchasing tools to break into a house is preparation, while using those tools to break the lock with the intention of stealing may amount to an attempt.

Punishment under Section 62 BNS:

Where no specific punishment for the attempt is provided, Section 62 permits imprisonment up to one-half of the longest term of imprisonment prescribed for the offence, or the corresponding proportion of life imprisonment, or the prescribed fine, or both.

Illustrations:

1. Attempt to Steal: A breaks open a box to steal jewellery but discovers that the box contains no jewellery. A may still be guilty of attempting to steal.

2. Attempt to Pick a Pocket: A puts a hand into Z's pocket intending to steal, but Z has nothing in the pocket. A may still be guilty of attempted theft. These examples are expressly provided under Section 62 BNS.

Important Case Law: State of Maharashtra v. Mohd. Yakub (1980)

Facts: The accused were involved in transporting silver ingots towards the seashore for illegal export. They began unloading the silver near a creek where a sea-craft was expected.

Judgment: The Supreme Court held that their conduct amounted to an attempt to export silver illegally rather than mere preparation.

Principle of Judgment: The Court explained that attempt begins where preparation ends. An act need not be the final step before completing the offence, but it must show a clear intention and be reasonably close to the commission of the offence.

Conclusion:

Thus, attempt is an important stage of criminal liability. It allows the law to punish a person who has moved beyond preparation and taken a sufficient step towards committing an offence, even when the offence is not completed. The applicable punishment depends on Section 62 BNS or any specific provision governing the particular attempt.
