Stages of Crime

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 1.3

A crime does not usually occur suddenly. It generally passes through different stages, starting from the formation of an idea and ending with the completion of the criminal act. In criminal law, there are four main stages of crime: Intention, Preparation, Attempt, and Accomplishment.

1. Intention (Mental Stage):

Intention is the first stage of a crime. It means that a person forms a decision or develops a desire to commit an offence. At this stage, the person only thinks about committing the crime and has not taken any external step towards it.

Example: A person decides in their mind to steal another person's property.

Legal Position: Mere intention is generally not punishable because no criminal act has been performed. However, the law may separately punish certain acts connected with a criminal plan.

2. Preparation:

Preparation is the second stage of crime. It means making arrangements and collecting the necessary things to commit an offence. At this stage, the person begins taking steps towards carrying out the criminal intention.

Example: A person purchases tools and arranges other things to break into a house for stealing money.

Legal Position: Preparation is generally not punishable, because the person may still change their mind. However, preparation for certain specified offences is punishable under law.

3. Attempt:

Attempt is the third stage of crime. It begins when a person goes beyond preparation and takes a direct step towards committing the offence. The crime may remain incomplete because of failure, interruption, or other circumstances.

Example: A person tries to break the lock of a house to steal property but is caught before entering the house.

Legal Position: An attempt to commit an offence is punishable under law where the legal requirements are satisfied. Section 62 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, provides a general punishment for attempts covered by that provision.

4. Accomplishment or Completion:

Accomplishment is the final stage of crime. It occurs when the person successfully completes the criminal act and all the required elements of the offence are present.

Example: A person enters a house, dishonestly takes another person's property, and completes the act of theft.

Legal Position: When the offence is completed, the person may be punished according to the applicable law, provided the necessary legal conditions for criminal liability are established.

Conclusion:

Thus, the four stages of crime are Intention, Preparation, Attempt, and Accomplishment. Mere intention and preparation are generally not punishable, subject to legal exceptions. An attempt and a completed offence may attract punishment according to the applicable law.
