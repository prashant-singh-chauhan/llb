Module 03 · General Exceptions

Young Age

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 3.4

Introduction:

Young age is an important general exception under criminal law. The law recognises that very young children may not have sufficient understanding to judge the nature and consequences of their actions. Therefore, the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) provides protection to children under Sections 20 and 21.

1. Section 20 – Act of a Child under Seven Years of Age:

Meaning: Section 20 provides that nothing is an offence which is done by a child under seven years of age. The law gives complete protection from criminal liability under this provision, regardless of whether the child understood the nature of the act.

Reason: The law presumes that a child below seven years does not possess the maturity necessary for criminal responsibility. This principle is known as doli incapax, meaning incapacity to commit a crime.

Illustration: A child aged six takes another person's toy without permission. The child cannot be held criminally liable for that act under Section 20 BNS.

2. Section 21 – Act of a Child above Seven and under Twelve Years of Age:

Meaning: Section 21 provides protection to a child above seven but under twelve years of age who has not attained sufficient maturity of understanding to judge the nature and consequences of their conduct on that particular occasion.

Reason: Children in this age group develop understanding at different rates. Therefore, the law does not automatically grant protection to every child in this age group. The child's maturity and understanding must be considered in relation to the particular act.

Illustration: A child aged nine takes another person's property without understanding the nature and consequences of the act. If the child has not attained sufficient maturity of understanding, Section 21 may protect the child from criminal liability.

Difference between Sections 20 and 21:

Section 20: Provides complete protection to a child under seven years of age.

Section 21: Provides conditional protection to a child above seven but under twelve years of age, depending on the child's maturity of understanding.

Important Case Law: Hiralal Mallick v. State of Bihar (1977)

Facts: Hiralal Mallick was twelve years old when he participated with his two elder brothers in an attack on a person. He used a sword and inflicted injuries on the victim.

Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld his conviction for voluntarily causing grievous hurt under Section 326 of the former Indian Penal Code. The Court considered his age and individual role in the incident.

Principle of Judgment: The Court emphasised that a young offender's age, understanding, role, and circumstances are important when assessing criminal responsibility and treatment. However, the accused did not receive the protection under Section 83 IPC because he was twelve years old and outside that provision's age limit.

Conclusion:

Thus, Sections 20 and 21 BNS protect children from criminal liability according to their age and maturity. Section 20 gives complete protection to children under seven, whereas Section 21 provides conditional protection to children above seven but under twelve who lack sufficient maturity of understanding. These provisions reflect the principle that criminal responsibility must take account of a child's age and mental development.
