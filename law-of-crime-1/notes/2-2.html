Module 02 · Inchoate Crime / Preliminary Offences

Conspiracy

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 2.2

Introduction:

Criminal conspiracy is an agreement between two or more persons to commit an illegal act or to do a legal act by illegal means. It is an important concept of criminal law and a form of inchoate crime, which means that criminal liability may arise before the intended offence is completed.

Legal Provision – Section 61 BNS:

Section 61 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, deals with criminal conspiracy. It corresponds to Sections 120A and 120B of the former Indian Penal Code, 1860. It defines criminal conspiracy and provides punishment for it.

Essential Elements of Criminal Conspiracy:

1. Two or More Persons: There must be an agreement between at least two persons. A person cannot conspire alone.

2. Agreement: The most important element is an agreement between the persons involved. They must agree to pursue an unlawful purpose or achieve a lawful purpose by unlawful means.

3. Illegal Act or Illegal Means: The agreement must relate to doing an illegal act or doing an otherwise legal act through illegal means.

4. Act in Furtherance: When the agreement is not to commit an offence itself, at least one additional act by a party in furtherance of the agreement is generally required. However, an agreement to commit an offence may itself constitute criminal conspiracy.

5. Common Object: The persons must share the object of the unlawful agreement. Their agreement and involvement must be established through evidence.

Punishment – Section 61(2) BNS:

1. Serious Offences: A person conspiring to commit an offence punishable with death, life imprisonment, or rigorous imprisonment for two years or more may be punished in the same manner as an abettor, where the BNS does not separately provide punishment for that conspiracy.

2. Other Conspiracies: Other criminal conspiracies are punishable with imprisonment up to six months, a fine, or both.

Illustration:

A and B agree to rob a bank. They discuss the plan and decide how to carry it out. Their agreement may constitute criminal conspiracy, even if the robbery is not ultimately completed, because the agreement is to commit an offence.

Important Case Law: Kehar Singh v. State (Delhi Administration) (1988)

Facts: This case arose from the assassination of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi on 31 October 1984. Kehar Singh and other accused were prosecuted for conspiracy connected with the assassination.

Judgment: The Supreme Court upheld Kehar Singh's conviction and held that the evidence established his participation in the conspiracy. However, Balbir Singh was acquitted because the evidence against him was insufficient.

Principle of Judgment: The Court explained that criminal conspiracy is usually formed in secrecy and may be proved through circumstantial evidence. Direct proof of an express agreement is not always necessary, but the evidence must establish that the accused shared and pursued the unlawful plan.

Conclusion:

Thus, criminal conspiracy under Section 61 BNS helps prevent and punish unlawful agreements. The prosecution must establish the agreement and the applicable legal requirements before a person can be held criminally liable.
