Module 03 · General Exceptions

Right of Private Defence

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 3.7

Introduction:

The right of private defence of person is an important general exception under criminal law. It allows a person to protect their own body or the body of another person against unlawful attacks. This right is protective, not a means of revenge, and must be exercised within the limits prescribed by law.

1. Legal Provision — Section 35 BNS

Section 35 provides:

“Every person has a right, subject to the restrictions contained in section 37, to defend— (a) his own body, and the body of any other person, against any offence affecting the human body.”

2. When Does the Right Extend to Causing Death? — Section 38 BNS

Section 38 states that the right of private defence of the body extends, subject to Section 37, to the voluntary causing of death or other harm to the assailant in the following circumstances:

(a) An assault that may reasonably cause the apprehension that death will otherwise result.

(b) An assault that may reasonably cause the apprehension that grievous hurt will otherwise result.

(c) An assault with the intention of committing rape.

(d) An assault with the intention of gratifying unnatural lust.

(e) An assault with the intention of kidnapping or abducting.

(f) An assault with the intention of wrongfully confining a person in circumstances creating a reasonable fear that recourse to public authorities for release will not be possible.

(g) Throwing or administering acid, or attempting to do so, where it may reasonably cause apprehension of grievous hurt.

3. When the Right Extends Only to Other Harm — Section 39 BNS

“If the offence be not of any of the descriptions specified in section 38, the right of private defence of the body does not extend to the voluntary causing of death to the assailant, but does extend, under the restrictions specified in section 37, to the voluntary causing to the assailant of any harm other than death.”

4. Commencement and Continuance — Section 40 BNS

Section 40 provides:

“The right of private defence of the body commences as soon as a reasonable apprehension of danger to the body arises from an attempt or threat to commit the offence though the offence may not have been committed; and it continues as long as such apprehension of danger to the body continues.”

Explanation: The right begins as soon as a reasonable apprehension of danger arises. A person need not wait for the actual attack. The right ends when the danger or reasonable apprehension of danger ends.

5. Circumstances in Which the Right Is Not Available — Section 37 BNS

Under Section 37, there is no right of private defence:

(a) Against an act of a public servant acting in good faith under colour of office, where the act does not reasonably create an apprehension of death or grievous hurt.

(b) Against an act done by the direction of such a public servant in similar circumstances.

(c) Where there is time to seek the protection of public authorities.

Section 37 further provides:

“The right of private defence in no case extends to the inflicting of more harm than it is necessary to inflict for the purpose of defence.”

Therefore, private defence cannot be used for revenge or to inflict unnecessary harm.

6. Important Case Law: Darshan Singh v. State of Punjab (2010)

Facts: Gurcharan Singh struck Darshan Singh's father on the head with a *gandasa* and then advanced towards Darshan. Fearing for his own life, Darshan fired his gun, killing Gurcharan Singh.

Judgment: The Supreme Court accepted the right of private defence and restored the trial court's acquittal.

Principle: A reasonable apprehension of danger is sufficient to exercise private defence. The force used must not be wholly disproportionate to the danger. The right is for protection, not retaliation.

Conclusion:

Thus, Sections 35, 37, 38, 39 and 40 BNS govern the right of private defence of the body. The right may extend to causing death only in the specified serious circumstances. It begins with reasonable apprehension of danger and continues only while that apprehension lasts, subject to the restrictions under Section 37.
