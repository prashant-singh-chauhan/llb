Module 04 · Specific Offences

Extortion

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 4.2 B

Extortion: Definition and Punishment
1. Definition of Extortion

Whoever intentionally puts any person in fear of any injury to that person or to any other person, and thereby dishonestly induces the person so put in fear to deliver to any person any property, valuable security, or anything signed or sealed which may be converted into a valuable security, commits the offence of extortion.

Essential Elements of Extortion:

1. Intentional Fear: The accused must intentionally put a person in fear of injury to that person or to another person.

2. Dishonest Inducement: The accused must dishonestly induce the person put in fear to deliver property or valuable security.

3. Delivery of Property or Valuable Security: The person must deliver property, valuable security, or a signed or sealed document capable of being converted into valuable security.

4. Connection Between Fear and Delivery: The delivery must result from the fear intentionally created by the accused.

2. Illustrations of Extortion

Illustration 1: Threat to Publish Defamatory Material

A threatens to publish a defamatory libel concerning Z unless Z gives him money. Z gives A the money because of the threat. A has committed extortion.

Illustration 2: Threat of Wrongful Confinement

A threatens Z that he will keep Z's child in wrongful confinement unless Z signs and delivers to A a promissory note binding Z to pay a certain sum of money. Z signs and delivers the note. A has committed extortion.

Illustration 3: Threat to Damage Property

A threatens to send club-men to plough up Z's field unless Z signs and delivers to B a bond binding Z, under a penalty, to deliver certain produce to B. Z signs and delivers the bond because of the threat. A has committed extortion.

Illustration 4: Threat of Grievous Hurt

A puts Z in fear of grievous hurt and dishonestly induces Z to sign or affix his seal to a blank paper and deliver it to A. Since the paper may be converted into a valuable security, A has committed extortion.

Illustration 5: Threat Through an Electronic Device

A sends Z a message through an electronic device stating, "Your child is in my possession, and will be put to death unless you send me one lakh rupees." Z gives A the money because of the threat. A has committed extortion.

3. Punishment for Extortion

Whoever commits extortion shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven years, or with fine, or with both.

4. Putting a Person in Fear of Injury to Commit Extortion

Whoever, in order to commit extortion, puts any person in fear, or attempts to put any person in fear, of any injury shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to two years, or with fine, or with both.

5. Putting a Person in Fear of Death or Grievous Hurt to Commit Extortion

Whoever, in order to commit extortion, puts or attempts to put any person in fear of death or grievous hurt to that person or to any other person shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to seven years and shall also be liable to fine.

6. Committing Extortion by Putting a Person in Fear of Death or Grievous Hurt

Whoever commits extortion by putting any person in fear of death or grievous hurt to that person or to any other person shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to ten years and shall also be liable to fine.

7. Putting a Person in Fear of Accusation of a Serious Offence to Commit Extortion

Whoever, in order to commit extortion, puts or attempts to put any person in fear of an accusation against that person or any other person of having committed or attempted to commit an offence punishable with death, imprisonment for life, or imprisonment for a term which may extend to ten years shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to ten years and shall also be liable to fine.

8. Committing Extortion by Putting a Person in Fear of Accusation of a Serious Offence

Whoever commits extortion by putting any person in fear of an accusation against that person or any other person of having committed or attempted to commit an offence punishable with death, imprisonment for life, or imprisonment for a term which may extend to ten years, or of having attempted to induce any other person to commit such an offence, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to ten years and shall also be liable to fine.

9. Summary of Punishments

Extortion: Imprisonment up to seven years, or fine, or both.

Putting or attempting to put a person in fear of injury to commit extortion: Imprisonment up to two years, or fine, or both.

Putting or attempting to put a person in fear of death or grievous hurt to commit extortion: Imprisonment up to seven years and liability to fine.

Committing extortion by putting a person in fear of death or grievous hurt: Imprisonment up to ten years and liability to fine.

Putting or attempting to put a person in fear of accusation of a serious offence to commit extortion: Imprisonment up to ten years and liability to fine.

Committing extortion through fear of accusation of a serious offence: Imprisonment up to ten years and liability to fine.

10. Conclusion

Extortion involves intentionally creating fear of injury and dishonestly using that fear to induce a person to deliver property, valuable security, or a document capable of becoming valuable security. The punishment depends on the nature of the threat and whether extortion is completed or only attempted. Threats involving death, grievous hurt, or accusations of serious offences attract more severe punishment.

When Does Extortion Amount to Dacoity?
1. Introduction

Extortion and dacoity are offences against property. Extortion involves dishonestly inducing a person to deliver property by intentionally putting that person in fear of injury. Dacoity is a more serious offence involving robbery committed or attempted jointly by a group of five or more persons. Under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS), the law distinguishes between ordinary extortion and extortion that amounts to robbery, which may further amount to dacoity when the required number of persons participate.

2. Meaning of Extortion

Extortion occurs when a person intentionally puts another person in fear of injury and dishonestly induces that person to deliver property, valuable security, or a document capable of being converted into valuable security.

Example: A threatens to harm B unless B gives him ₹50,000. B gives the money because of the threat. A commits extortion.

3. When Does Extortion Amount to Robbery?

Under Section 309 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, extortion amounts to robbery when the offender is present and, by putting the victim in fear of instant death, instant hurt, or instant wrongful restraint, induces the victim to deliver the property then and there.

Thus, the following conditions must be satisfied:

1. Fear of Immediate Harm: The victim must be put in fear of instant death, instant hurt, or instant wrongful restraint.

2. Presence of the Offender: The offender must be present when the threat is made and the property is delivered.

3. Immediate Delivery: The victim must be induced to deliver the property then and there because of that fear.

If these conditions are satisfied, extortion amounts to robbery.

4. When Does Robbery Amount to Dacoity?

Under Section 310 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, robbery amounts to dacoity when five or more persons conjointly commit or attempt to commit robbery, or when the total number of persons conjointly committing or attempting robbery, together with those present and aiding them, is five or more.

Example: Five persons surround a shopkeeper, threaten to kill him immediately, and force him to hand over cash. If the legal requirements of robbery are fulfilled, their joint act amounts to dacoity.

5. Important Distinction

Every extortion does not amount to robbery, and every robbery does not amount to dacoity. Extortion becomes robbery only when the statutory conditions concerning immediate fear, the offender's presence, and immediate delivery are fulfilled. Robbery becomes dacoity when five or more persons participate in the manner specified by law.

6. Conclusion

Extortion amounts to dacoity only when it first satisfies the legal requirements of robbery and the robbery is committed or attempted jointly by at least five persons, including persons present and aiding the offence as specified by law. Therefore, the immediate threat and delivery requirements distinguish robbery from ordinary extortion, while the participation of five or more persons distinguishes dacoity from robbery.
