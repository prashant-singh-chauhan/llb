Concept of Crime

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 1.1

What is Crime ?

Crime is an act or omission that is prohibited by law and punishable by the State. In simple words, crime means doing something forbidden by law or failing to do something required by law, where such conduct is treated as a criminal offence.

Crime is considered a wrong against society because it disturbs public peace, affects the rights of individuals, or threatens the safety and security of people. Examples of crimes include theft, robbery, cheating, assault and murder. A person may also commit a crime by failing to perform a legal duty, where the law treats such failure as an offence.

For an act to be considered a crime, it must come within the definition of an offence established by law, and the necessary legal conditions must be satisfied. Every immoral or socially unacceptable act is not necessarily a crime. It becomes a crime only when the law prohibits and makes it punishable.

Crime is different from a civil wrong. A civil wrong generally concerns a private dispute and may result in compensation, whereas a crime may lead to criminal proceedings and punishment, such as imprisonment, a fine, or another penalty permitted by law.
