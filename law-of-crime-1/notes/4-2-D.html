Module 04 · Specific Offences

Dacoity

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 4.2 D

Dacoity under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)
1. Introduction

Dacoity is a serious offence against property. It involves robbery committed or attempted jointly by five or more persons. The law punishes dacoity strictly because it involves a group of persons working together to commit robbery. Dacoity is defined under Section 310 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS).

2. Definition of Dacoity

According to Section 310(1) of the BNS, when five or more persons jointly commit or attempt to commit robbery, or when the total number of persons committing or attempting robbery together with persons present and helping them is five or more, every person committing, attempting or helping in that robbery is said to commit dacoity.

In simple words, when five or more persons participate in committing or attempting robbery, including those who are present and assist the offence, it amounts to dacoity.

3. Essential Ingredients of Dacoity

1. Commission or Attempt of Robbery: The act must involve committing or attempting to commit robbery.

2. Five or More Persons: At least five persons must be involved. The number includes persons committing or attempting the robbery and persons present and assisting them.

3. Joint Participation: The persons must act together in committing or attempting the robbery. A person who is present and assists the offence may also be liable, even if that person does not personally take the property.

4. Assistance in the Offence: Persons helping the group during the robbery or its attempt may be included in the total number, provided the conditions of Section 310(1) are satisfied.

4. Illustrations of Dacoity

Illustration 1: Five persons enter a jewellery shop together, threaten the shopkeeper and take jewellery. Their act amounts to dacoity.

Illustration 2: Four persons enter a house to commit robbery, while a fifth person stands outside and helps them escape. If the statutory requirements are satisfied, all five may be liable for dacoity.

Illustration 3: Four persons jointly commit robbery without any other person present and aiding them. Their act is robbery, but it does not amount to dacoity because the required number of persons is not reached.

5. Punishment for Dacoity

Section 310 provides different punishments for dacoity and related acts:

1. Dacoity [Section 310(2)]: Imprisonment for life, or rigorous imprisonment for up to ten years, and fine.

2. Dacoity with Murder [Section 310(3)]: If any one of the five or more persons committing dacoity commits murder during the offence, every person involved in that dacoity may be punished with death, imprisonment for life, or rigorous imprisonment for not less than ten years, and fine.

3. Preparation for Dacoity [Section 310(4)]: Rigorous imprisonment for up to ten years and fine.

4. Assembly for Dacoity [Section 310(5)]: A person who is one of five or more persons assembled for the purpose of committing dacoity may be punished with rigorous imprisonment for up to seven years and fine.

5. Belonging to a Gang of Dacoits [Section 310(6)]: A person belonging to a gang associated with the habitual commission of dacoity may be punished with imprisonment for life, or rigorous imprisonment for up to ten years, and fine.

6. Difference Between Robbery and Dacoity

Robbery may be committed by one person or several persons. Dacoity requires at least five persons participating in the manner prescribed by law. Therefore, every dacoity is robbery, but every robbery is not dacoity.

7. Conclusion

Dacoity under Section 310 of the BNS is a serious property offence involving robbery committed or attempted jointly by five or more persons, including persons present and assisting the offence. The law also punishes preparation, assembly for the purpose of dacoity, dacoity involving murder, and membership of a gang habitually committing dacoity. These provisions aim to protect people and their property from organised robbery.
