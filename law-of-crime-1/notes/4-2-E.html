Module 04 · Specific Offences

Dishonest Misappropriation of Property

Law of Crimes – I · Topic 4.2 E

Criminal Misappropriation of Property under Section 314 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023
1. Introduction

Criminal misappropriation of property is an offence against property under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS). It occurs when a person dishonestly misappropriates or converts movable property for personal use. The offence is defined under Section 314 of the BNS. The section also explains the legal position relating to property taken by mistake, property used temporarily, and property found by a person.

2. Definition and Punishment

Section 314 provides that whoever dishonestly misappropriates or converts to his own use any movable property shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which shall not be less than six months but which may extend to two years, and with fine.

3. Essential Ingredients

1. Movable Property: The property involved must be movable, such as money, a book, jewellery, or a valuable document.

2. Dishonest Intention: The person must act dishonestly, intending to obtain wrongful gain or cause wrongful loss.

3. Misappropriation or Conversion: The person must dishonestly use the property for personal benefit or deal with it as his own without lawful justification.

4. Dishonest Use May Arise Later: The property may initially come into the person's possession honestly or by mistake. Criminal misappropriation occurs when the person subsequently decides to use it dishonestly.

4. Statutory Illustrations

Illustration (a): A takes property belonging to Z out of Z's possession, in good faith believing at the time when he takes it, that the property belongs to himself. A is not guilty of theft; but if A, after discovering his mistake, dishonestly appropriates the property to his own use, he is guilty of an offence under this section.

Illustration (b): A, being on friendly terms with Z, goes into Z's library in Z's absence, and takes away a book without Z's express consent. Here, if A was under the impression that he had Z's implied consent to take the book for the purpose of reading it, A has not committed theft. But, if A afterwards sells the book for his own benefit, he is guilty of an offence under this section.

Illustration (c): A and B, being joint owners of a horse. A takes the horse out of B's possession, intending to use it. Here, as A has a right to use the horse, he does not dishonestly misappropriate it. But, if A sells the horse and appropriates the whole proceeds to his own use, he is guilty of an offence under this section.

5. Explanation 1: Temporary Misappropriation

A dishonest misappropriation for a time only is a misappropriation within the meaning of this section.

Statutory Illustration: A finds a Government promissory note belonging to Z, bearing a blank endorsement. A, knowing that the note belongs to Z, pledges it with a banker as a security for a loan, intending at a future time to restore it to Z. A has committed an offence under this section.

6. Explanation 2: Misappropriation of Found Property

A person who finds property not in the possession of any other person and takes it to protect it for, or restore it to, the owner does not act dishonestly merely by taking it. However, the finder commits an offence if he appropriates the property to his own use when he knows or has the means of discovering the owner, or before making reasonable efforts to discover and notify the owner and allowing a reasonable time for the owner to claim it.

What amounts to reasonable efforts and a reasonable time depends upon the facts of each case. The finder need not know the owner's identity. It is sufficient that, at the time of appropriation, the finder does not believe the property to be his own or does not honestly believe that the real owner cannot be found.

7. Statutory Illustrations under Explanation 2

Illustration (a): A finds a rupee on the high road, not knowing to whom the rupee belongs, A picks up the rupee. Here A has not committed the offence defined in this section.

Illustration (b): A finds a letter on the road, containing a bank-note. From the direction and contents of the letter he learns to whom the note belongs. He appropriates the note. He is guilty of an offence under this section.

Illustration (c): A finds a cheque payable to bearer. He can form no conjecture as to the person who has lost the cheque. But the name of the person, who has drawn the cheque, appears. A knows that this person can direct him to the person in whose favour the cheque was drawn. A appropriates the cheque without attempting to discover the owner. He is guilty of an offence under this section.

Illustration (d): A sees Z drop his purse with money in it. A picks up the purse with the intention of restoring it to Z, but afterwards appropriates it to his own use. A has committed an offence under this section.

Illustration (e): A finds a purse with money, not knowing to whom it belongs; he afterwards discovers that it belongs to Z, and appropriates it to his own use. A is guilty of an offence under this section.

Illustration (f): A finds a valuable ring, not knowing to whom it belongs. A sells it immediately without attempting to discover the owner. A is guilty of an offence under this section.

8. Conclusion

Criminal misappropriation occurs when a person dishonestly uses movable property for personal benefit. The dishonest intention may arise after the property is obtained, and even temporary dishonest use may amount to an offence. Section 314 of the BNS protects the rights of property owners and provides punishment for dishonest misappropriation.
